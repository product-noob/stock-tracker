## The week in review

The week the AI trade stopped being a rotation and became a rout.

Monday was the policy day. The Commerce Department issued an interim final rule (RIN 0694-AK50) requiring licences for remote access to advanced AI chips by firms in China and other D:5 countries — data centres anywhere in the world need a licence if they supply more than 12 million aggregate TPP (roughly 750 H100s / ~330 B200s) of compute to Chinese-headquartered firms — plus licence requirements for advanced-chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, named as diversion risks. The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. Existing operators have 90 days to get licences; it takes effect on Federal Register publication. Asia had already set the tone overnight: Taiwan's Taiex fell 6.03% — its second-largest point drop on record — with TSMC Taipei −5.74%, the Kospi −3.59% and the Nikkei −2.42%. The S&P fell 1.92% and the Nasdaq 2.49%, and every name in the universe was red: **TSM −6.34%**, **ASML −5.91%**, **NVDA −4.12%**, **GOOGL −4.07%**, **AMZN −3.34%**, **META −2.38%**, **MSFT −1.12%**, **AAPL −0.98%**. Analysts cut Nvidia targets into the print (UBS Buy $300 → $240, Mizuho Outperform $250 → $220, TD Cowen Buy $275 → $200, CLSA $255). November Empire State manufacturing printed 7.8 vs ~17 expected — a sharp deceleration — and Fed Chair Warsh's noon remarks were all AI, no monetary policy: no hint on the December hike the market has fully priced.

Tuesday was the earnings day — and the print was extraordinary and the reaction was nothing. Nvidia reported after the close: **revenue $115.84B, +103% year over year** (consensus ~$109.1B, guide $108B ±2%), **adjusted EPS $2.68** (consensus ~$2.47–2.49), **data-centre revenue $107.6B, +110%**, a **Q4 guide of ~$130B at the midpoint** ($127.4–132.6B, vs ~$125.3B consensus) and a **new $150B buyback authorisation**. The stock rose ~2.4% after hours. Earlier in the session Jensen Huang broke his silence in his first statement since resigning, posting on X that he owns his mistakes, will cooperate with the board's independent investigation, and would be "honoured" to return as CEO if the board decides it is in shareholders', employees', the company's and America's best interest. NVDA popped ~5% off its $150.33 intraday low on the statement and closed −0.44% at $155.46. The rest of the tape waited for the print and fell anyway: S&P −1.03%, Nasdaq −0.74%, Dow −1.64%. **TSM −3.68%** broke the $368 support and tagged $353.50; **MSFT −2.27%** broke $500 intraday (low $496.51) as preliminary findings on the global Microsoft 365 outage landed — a bug undetected since July, routine traffic rerouting on November 11 cascading into Outlook/Teams/Copilot failures. October retail sales (+0.2% headline, control group +0.6% vs +0.4% forecast) offered no relief and no damage; St. Louis Fed President Musalem said several rate increases may be needed before spring 2027. Disney beat and faded; Home Depot beat comps and fell ~2% on a cautious guide and a CEO succession.

Wednesday was the split, priced violently — in the wrong direction for the name that mattered most. The FOMC minutes of the October 27–28 meeting (released 2:00 PM ET) showed "almost all" officials judged further rate increases would likely be appropriate; some favoured a hike in October, a few who backed the hold called it a close call, and "a couple" noted an increase of more than 25bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data proved unfavourable. The AI buildout featured prominently — officials called it a material source of inflation risk and flagged financial-stability concerns. Before the minutes, Governor Waller had said the Fed may need to "shock people" with a half-point hike at the December 8–9 meeting, or clearly signal a faster pace of hikes in 2027 — inflation is "headed in the wrong direction" and broadening, with ~70% of core services items rising above 3%. NY Fed President Williams countered that another increase would probably be needed but pushed back on an aggressive path. **NVDA opened +2.4% at $159.19, touched $159.70, then reversed all of it and more to close −2.89% at $150.96, $1.60 above the November 12 52-week low of $148.80.** The analyst tape split with it: Piper Sandler cut its target to $230 from $300 (Overweight kept), Jefferies $300 → $250, Wells Fargo $300 → $250, UBS $240 → $220, Barclays $275 → $210, DA Davidson $300 → $200, Evercore $186 → $184, Summit Insights downgraded to Hold — while JPMorgan raised to $230 from $215, Wedbush raised to $293 from $275 and Morgan Stanley upgraded to Buy. The day's winner was the exception that proved the rule: **AMZN +3.82% to $267.74**, on Blackstone agreeing to buy a 75% stake in two AWS data-centre joint ventures for ~$5B (13 halls, 647 MW), AWS keeping 25% and leasing back — a capital-light answer to the ~$220B 2026 capex plan — plus Nvidia's print showing the largest cloud companies paid it $60.1B in Q3 (+71% year over year), validating hyperscaler AI spend, and a UBS Buy initiation at $340. The Dow's −1.12% was healthcare, not tech: UnitedHealth plunged ~9% after the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services proposed a rule widening audits of Medicare and Medicaid payments. Anthropic priced its IPO at **$83 a share**, above the $70–78 range — 980M shares, ~$81.3B, the largest IPO on record.

Thursday was the debut that didn't save the tape. Anthropic (ANT) opened at **$111.50 (+34% vs the $83 IPO price)**, briefly touched ~$115 and a ~$2T valuation, then slid to close at **$97.17 (+17%)**. The whole tape opened +1–2% and reversed within the first hour; by noon the S&P had erased a +1.3% early gain, and 9 of 10 S&P stocks fell. The drivers were the same four that have framed the month: the Fed as brake (Vice Chair Jefferson said the AI buildout is adding to inflation; Chicago Fed's Goolsbee warned the Fed's job is to prevent the AI investment surge from overheating the economy and that tariff inflation looks more persistent than expected); soft morning data (Philly Fed manufacturing fell to 6.3, a six-month low, vs ~20 expected, with prices-received at its highest since June 2022; jobless claims 203k vs ~198k expected — weak activity, sticky prices); semis taking a second hit (AMD fell ~7–8% after short-seller Hunterbrook alleged China export-control violations); and earnings disappointments outside tech (Walmart beat and raised guidance but fell ~1.4% on a soft Q4 implied EPS; UnitedHealth extended the managed-care selloff on the CMS clawback proposal; Williams-Sonoma −13.5% on a revenue miss). The universe: **AMZN −3.87%** (worst in the group, erasing all of Wednesday's gain), **GOOGL −3.46%** (the cruellest round trip — +4.2% at the open on the Anthropic stake plus the Gemini 4 Neon coding-agent launch, then a ~$20 slide from the $295.43 high to the day low), **NVDA −3.30%** (a fresh 52-week low, below the November 12 $148.80), **META −3.27%** (under the $600 line), **TSM −2.90%**, **ASML −2.80%**, **MSFT −1.97%** (ex-dividend day, $0.98), **AAPL −0.92%** (most resilient, again). The day's other news: California Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia, investigating whether advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether Nvidia misled investors about the risks; Moody's cut Nvidia's credit outlook to Stable from Positive (Aa1 rating affirmed); the Wall Street Journal reported the board has formally retained Heidrick & Struggles to run the CEO search and has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a possible successor. The S&P fell ~2.0%, the Nasdaq ~2.3%, the Dow ~2.2% — the sixth straight down day for the S&P, its longest slide since April 2024.

Friday was capitulation. Reuters reported in the afternoon that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia — the Wall Street Journal had first reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating the company, and Friday's report added the accounting angle, with possible focus areas including geographic revenue reporting and customer financing/guarantees (~$118B total, including ~$105B of credit support for a single data-centre campus, disclosed on the earnings call). **NVDA fell 14.01% to $125.53 — a new 52-week low ($124.50 intraday), its seventh straight losing day, on 633M shares, ~2.9x average volume, erasing ~$491B of market value in a single session.** The stock is now ~48% below its October 6 high of $243.37, down ~23% on the week — its worst week since 2008 — and its seventh straight weekly drop, the longest since 2008. The whole complex went with it: **TSM −9.48% to $318.71** (the $350–360 scale-in zone now ~$30 behind), **ASML −6.63% to $1,266.22** (Amsterdam −9.33%), **META −5.93% to $560.84**, **AMZN −4.10% to $246.82**, **MSFT −2.77% to $484.50** (breaking the bottom of its $488–517 chop), **GOOGL −2.70% to $265.29** (a new 52-week low at $264.22 intraday, but the most resilient name for a second day), **AAPL −1.81% to $342.91** (most resilient in the group, again). Anthropic fell 8.50% to **$88.91**, its low $83.05 — five cents above the IPO price. The benchmarks: **S&P 500 −3.70% to 6,910.39** (correction confirmed, ~10% below its record high; seventh straight down day; week −8.6%, the largest weekly loss since March 2025), **Nasdaq Composite −5.28% to 22,617.73** (negative for 2026; the second-largest one-day points drop in history, −1,261; week −10.0%, the largest weekly drop since March 2020), **Nasdaq-100 −5.6%** (week −10.4%, worst since 2020), **Dow −1.93% to 48,147.33** (correction confirmed; week −8.0%). The VIX closed at 35.89 (+26.2% on the day, intraday high 40.03 — its highest close since April 7, 2025, and +86% on the week). The 10-year Treasury fell 10.4bp to ~4.95% — the largest daily drop since October 22 — and the 2-year fell 12.6bp to ~4.66% even with a December hike still priced in: a genuine safe-haven bid, not a rates repricing. Gold fell 2.02% to $3,548.53 and silver 2.54% — Treasuries were the only haven that worked. Asia set the tone overnight: Japan and Korea −5%+, Taiwan −17% for the week (biggest since 1990), MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan −8.3% for the week (worst since COVID, March 2020). Intuit fell 12.77% after beating Q1 but cutting FY27 guidance and announcing the ~$3.68B sale of Mailchimp — the AI/software derating now hitting names with no Nvidia link at all. Ross Stores beat, then trimmed its Q4 guide; Gap also cut its outlook. After hours, the universe bounced 1–3.5% (NVDA $130.01, +3.57%) — thin-volume Friday-night trading, not a turn; every prior bounce this month has been sold.

For the week, the group did not split along the line drawn two weeks ago — it fell together, and the only distinction left was how far. **NVDA −22.92%**, **TSM −20.07%**, **ASML −15.31%**, **META −12.60%**, **GOOGL −10.31%**, **MSFT −7.52%**, **AMZN −7.43%**, **AAPL −3.75%**. The indices still tell the story in one line: the S&P and the Dow both confirmed corrections, the Nasdaq-100 had its worst week since 2020, and a company that reported revenue up 103% and guided to $130B next quarter fell 14% in a day. The market is pricing leadership, trust and rates — not demand. Nvidia's market value (~$3.03T) is now below Alphabet's (~$3.26T) for the first time in the recent era, and Microsoft passed Nvidia in market value for the first time since June 2025 a week earlier. The one green shoot is the after-hours bounce and an oversold tape (VIX above 30, S&P and Nasdaq RSI both into oversold territory) — but a bounce is not a bottom while NVDA keeps printing new lows.

### NVDA — Nvidia · $125.53 · P/E ~13.5 · fwd P/E ~8.7 · consensus ~$244 (Buy) — stale; live cluster ~$170–230

The week's story, and a valuation reset that has now become a governance crisis. The stock is **−22.92% on the week, −43.5% over the past month, −48.4% below the October 6 high of $243.37, and −32.7% over the past year** — its worst week since 2008 and its seventh straight weekly drop, the longest since 2008. The forward P/E has compressed to ~8.7x — down from more than 32x a year ago — which is either the cheapest the best AI asset has ever looked, or the market pricing a company whose founder premium, the thing that justified the multiple, just walked out the door and whose accounting is now under SEC scrutiny. The numbers in the business have not changed, and Tuesday's print proved it again: Q3 FY27 revenue $115.84B (+103% year over year), adjusted EPS $2.68, data-centre revenue $107.6B (+110%), GAAP gross margin 74.8%, Q4 guide ~$130B ±3% (a 14-week quarter, assuming no data-centre compute revenue from China) with gross margin 71–72%, Vera Rubin NVL72 shipped, supply commitments up to $390B (from $279B), a cloud backlog of ~$2.5T per earnings-call write-ups, $28.1B returned to shareholders in Q3 and ~$227.3B of buyback authorisation remaining; the next dividend of $0.25 is payable December 23 (record December 2). The balance sheet is the bull case's anchor ($62.47B cash, $38.86B debt, $23.61B net cash, trailing free cash flow $127.01B, a $150B buyback authorised in September). What changed is everything around the numbers: the California AG's investigative subpoena (Thursday), the Reuters report that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues (Friday), Moody's cutting the credit outlook to Stable from Positive (Aa1 affirmed), the board formally retaining Heidrick & Struggles and approaching Arm CEO Rene Haas, Treasury Secretary Bessent's November 15 demand for an outside CEO (answers due ~November 25), President Trump publicly backing Huang's return (the board says it does not intend to reinstate him), Huang's December 1 closed-door House testimony, and the DOJ reviewing the planned $12.9B Hugging Face acquisition. **Street this week:** the post-earnings wave cut targets again — Piper Sandler $300 → $230, Jefferies $300 → $250, Wells Fargo $300 → $250, UBS $240 → $220, Morgan Stanley $267 → $260 (upgraded to Buy), Barclays $275 → $210, DA Davidson $300 → $200, Evercore $186 → $184, Summit Insights downgraded to Hold — with two raises (JPMorgan $215 → $230, Wedbush $275 → $293) and William Blair initiating at Outperform on Friday. ## Macro watch

**The week the December hike stayed a near-certainty — and the 50bp question stayed alive, again.** The October 27–28 FOMC minutes (released Wednesday) showed "almost all" officials judged further rate increases would likely be appropriate; some favoured a hike in October, a few who backed the hold called it a close call, and "a couple" noted an increase of more than 25bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data proved unfavourable. The AI buildout featured prominently — officials called it a material source of inflation risk and flagged financial-stability concerns, with staff warning on AI-related debt (debt-financed construction, off-balance-sheet leases, circular financing) as a risk, though banks were judged resilient. Governor Waller, the hawkish outlier, said the Fed may need to "shock people" with a half-point hike in December, or clearly signal a faster pace of hikes in 2027; Vice Chair Jefferson said the AI buildout is adding to inflation; Chicago Fed President Goolsbee warned the Fed's job is to prevent the AI investment surge from overheating the economy and that tariff inflation looks more persistent than expected. NY Fed President Williams countered that current conditions do not call for a "big step" in December — but the AI boom and the wealth effect from rising stocks could justify further hikes next year. St. Louis Fed President Musalem said rates could rise as much as a full percentage point by spring 2027. Governor Lisa Cook said on Friday that, based on CPI/PPI already released, October PCE was estimated at 3.8% year over year and core PCE at 3.2%. The market still prices a December hike of some size as a near-certainty — central estimate **~95% for 25bp, ~5% for 50bp** (CME FedWatch, November 18–20 snapshots; post-minutes prints ran as high as ~97% for 25bp). A hike repricing lifts the discount rate on long-duration growth cash flows and raises the hurdle for AI capex funded at the margin — the direct channel for every name in this letter. With the 10-year at ~4.95% after falling ~20bp on the week on a safe-haven bid, the equity risk premium is thin: the S&P 500 forward P/E was 17.7x on November 5, an earnings yield of ~5.65% against a 5.13% 10-year — a ~52bp premium, the lowest cushion in years. The offset, for this universe: the same inflation print reflects resilient nominal demand, and NVDA/TSM/ASML earnings are driven more by AI capex orders than by rates in the near term. The December 9 SEP and dot plot — not just the 25 vs 50bp call — is what will reset the 2027 path for these names.
