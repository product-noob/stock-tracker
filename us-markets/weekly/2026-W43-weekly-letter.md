## The week in review

The week a good earnings print got sold, and the market told you exactly what it is pricing.

Monday was repair. After the AI-debt washout of the prior week (Nasdaq −4.5%, the SOX's eight-day slide), the tape bounced on US–Iran de-escalation talk pulling oil back, and the rebound was led by the names with fresh catalysts: **AMZN +4.95%** — Morgan Stanley naming it Top Pick and raising its target to $350, plus Reuters' report of **TorchNeuron**, AWS's native PyTorch backend for Trainium, open-sourced at the PyTorch Conference — and **TSM +1.75%** on the weekend's Samsung-on-TSMC-3nm NVHBM story. **META fell a fifth straight day** into its Wednesday print, dipping below $700 intraday.

Tuesday was the split tape: the Dow advanced while tech lagged, Asia's chip selloff (KOSPI −3.73%) set the tone, and **META closed below $700 for a sixth straight down day**. The week's hinge was now hours away.

Wednesday delivered the print — and, briefly, the relief. **META's Q3 was the double beat the de-risked tape needed: revenue $64.6B (+26% y/y) vs ~$63.3B consensus, GAAP EPS $3.83 after the $9.89B state attorneys-general settlement accrual (about $7.40 clean), Q4 revenue guided to $73–76B (midpoint above the Street's ~$73.6B), and 2026 capex narrowed to $135–145B (low end raised from $130B).** Free cash flow was **−$2.12B — the first negative quarter since the IPO, but far better than the feared −$9.4B.** Shares rose ~3% after hours. The rest of the tape rallied too: SpaceX +7–8% on its first profitable quarter (revenue $14.58B, +161%), Super Micro +9–10% on a strong prelim guide, **NVDA +2.46%** on Huang's **DSX POD** modular Vera Rubin factories at GTC Berlin, **ASML +2.73%** on Musk's Terafab-acceleration comment. **GOOGL was the day's laggard for a second straight session (−1.30%)** — no company news, just drift.

Thursday was the reckoning. The market read META's print overnight and decided the print wasn't the story — the bill was. **META fell 7.04%** as the tape repriced three things at once: the capex low-end raise, the **new non-voting Class C shares** (one-for-one dividend, effective January 19, 2027 — an effective two-for-one split that also gives Meta a fresh capital-raising vehicle), and the **$25B New Mexico privacy judgment** (appeal planned). Lam Research reported a record quarter and fell ~9–10% on a supply-constrained December outlook, dragging Applied Materials ~−6% and the whole equipment group. **The Nasdaq fell ~2.1%, the Nasdaq-100 ~2.9%, the S&P ~1.4%; the SOX fell 5.6% — roughly $616B of semiconductor market value at risk, its lowest since July 29.** Oil compounded it (Brent ~$112, five-month high, on Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia) while the 10-year slipped below 5% intraday for the first time since late September. **AAPL (+1.15%) was the lone green name** — the safe-cash-flow bid, again.

Friday was two tapes at once. Chips bounced (SOX ~+1%, Nasdaq Composite +0.21%) while the oil shock hit everything else (Brent **$116.27, a five-month high**, on Houthi attacks including the Yanbu Aramco facility and a UAE tanker spill; the Dow −0.9%, the S&P −0.14% with 348 of 503 names red). The SpaceX wireless-carrier story — Bloomberg reporting talks with cellular-infrastructure firms — knocked T, Verizon and T-Mobile down 3–4.7% premarket and lifted Crown Castle +8.6% and American Tower +6.1%. **TSM (+1.11%) and MSFT (+0.64%) led the universe; GOOGL fell a sixth straight day, sitting on $320, ~6% below its 200-day average and below a $4T market cap; META (−0.98%) closed below its 2026 opening price (~$648.74) — the year's gains gone.**

For the week, the split was brutal and legible: **AAPL (+1.2%) and TSM (+0.8%) the only gainers; META (−9.4%) the worst week of any name in the group this quarter; GOOGL (−5.9%) and ASML (−4.5%) the other big losers; MSFT (−0.9%), AMZN (−0.8%) and NVDA (−1.5%) drifting.** The week's thesis, in one line: **the market is no longer asking whether AI spending is real — it is asking who pays for it, and it sold the payer (META) hardest, the supplier (ASML) second, and the one name that doesn't need to pay (AAPL) not at all.**

## Macro watch
