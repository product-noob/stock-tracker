## The week in review

The week the AI trade stopped being one trade.

Monday was repair, and it looked like a turning point. A better-than-expected ISM manufacturing print (55.5, a three-month high) and oil plunging on hopes for more US–Iran diplomacy time lifted the broad tape — but the universe split from the open. **AMZN +2.20%** on a Phillip Securities upgrade (Moderate Buy to Strong Buy, target $330), while **AAPL −1.83%** faded into its print, **MSFT −2.83%** gave back the morning bounce, and the chip names leaked. After the close, **Apple reported a record fiscal Q4: revenue $114.48B (+12%), EPS $2.10, both beating consensus** — and the stock fell anyway, for a second straight session on Tuesday, because of the guide: December-quarter gross margin of **45–46%, down from 47.9%**, with management calling memory-price increases unprecedented and worsening. The quarter was fine; the margin trajectory is the debate.

Tuesday was the rebound that wasn't. Election Day brought a chip-led bounce — **NVDA +2.29%**, **MSFT +2.74%**, **META +2.36%** — while **AAPL −1.90%** digested its margin warning and **GOOGL +0.37%** lagged the tape. After the close, AMD reported a blowout Q3 with a greater-than-80% 2027 growth outlook, and the polls began closing. Nothing in the prices yet said what was coming.

Wednesday was the split. The midterm sweep — Democrats taking the Senate and House, plus state houses and governorships — was first read as gridlock, and the market paid up for cash flow while selling the buildout. **AMZN +6.12%, MSFT +6.68% to a record high ($559.15 intraday), AAPL +5.31% to a record, GOOGL +3.18%, META +1.44%** — against **NVDA −3.17%, TSM −2.24%, ASML −3.60%**. The thesis, in the market's own words: a Democratic Congress constrains data-centre construction (permits, power, local restrictions — the Texas governor-elect pledged a moratorium; voters in Ohio, Illinois, Maine and Florida passed local bans), which eases the hyperscalers' capex burden and chokes the suppliers' order books. The Dow rose 3.03%, its best day since April 2025. The analyst split on Apple mirrored the tape: Goldman Sachs set a $380 target; DZ Bank cut Hold to Sell at $300.

Thursday was the reckoning — the other side of the same thesis, priced violently. The SOX fell **12.25%** and the Nasdaq-100 **6.35%**, the worst one-day drops for both since March 2020; the Nasdaq Composite entered correction, more than 10% off its October 6 record, and closed below its 200-day moving average for the first time since April. **NVDA −9.26%**, erasing more than $440B of market value in a day; **TSM −10.78%**; **ASML −9.33%**. Qualcomm's warning shot — handset-chip revenue down 26% year over year — dragged the whole semiconductor complex with it, and the "AI bubble" debate resurfaced with financing risk around AI infrastructure debt back in focus. UBS strategists estimate nearly one-third of the computing power projected to come online in the US in 2028 is now at risk. No universe name reported, guided, or cut an order all day. The numbers were politics, positioning, and a demand scare — not fundamentals.

Friday was the relief bounce, and it was only a bounce. A blowout October jobs report — **+165,000 payrolls vs ~65,000 expected, unemployment down to 4.1%** — gave dip buyers a fundamental excuse, and chips led the recovery: **TSM +6.33%, ASML +5.02%, NVDA +2.78%**. But the rally faded all afternoon; **MSFT −0.99%** and **AAPL −0.52%** slipped into the red, META gave back nearly half its gain, and the recovery is still only a fraction of Thursday's losses. The same strong jobs print pushed December Fed hike odds to ~76–77% — the new overhang for everything priced on cheap capital. The M6 MacBook Pro, M6 iMac and first touchscreen MacBook Pro went on sale Friday and the stock didn't care; the tape, not the product, is driving Apple right now.

For the week, the group split exactly along the cash-flow line. **AMZN +7.88%** was the week's winner; **GOOGL +1.76%** and **MSFT +1.67%** held small gains; **META −0.79%** and **AAPL −0.65%** were near flat; the buildout suppliers were crushed — **TSM −7.56%, NVDA −8.79%, ASML −9.01%**. Bloomberg's weekend analysis frames it as a genuine reassessment, not a one-day panic: NVDA now trades at ~13x estimated profits over the next 12 months, down from more than 32x a year ago. The week's thesis, in one line: **the market stopped debating whether AI spending is too high and started pricing in whether the buildout itself slows — and it grades every name on free cash flow, with MSFT's +$18.6B still the only passing mark among the hyperscalers.**

## Fundamentals

### GOOGL — Alphabet · $284.77 · P/E ~14.9 · fwd P/E ~20.7 · consensus ~$400 (Strong Buy)

No fresh company catalyst in US press all week — the quietest holding, and the cheapest-looking one on the board. The Q3 print from October 28 is still the frame: revenue $127.58B (+25% y/y) vs ~$128.2B consensus, the first revenue miss since Q4 2022; EPS $2.12 vs ~$3.02, mostly non-operating (an ~$8.7B unrealised loss on equity securities plus a $5.2B cash EU antitrust fine payment). Underneath, Google Cloud more than doubled (+103%) to $30.7B with a $561B backlog, and search query volume grew at its fastest in five years. The two real issues: search revenue growth slowed to ~12% (management blamed currency, softer Asian retail ads and lower monetisation in AI Mode), and cash — Q3 capex $51.4B, free cash flow −$9.07B, 2026 capex $195–205B kept, 2027 spend guided to rise "significantly." Alphabet funded the build with $12.4B of stock sales and $28.7B of new debt in the quarter; a second euro-bond sale was reported in preparation on Monday. **Street this week:** Wells Fargo set a $365 target (Nov 2); the post-print cluster (Bernstein $325, JPMorgan $375, Citi $375, BofA $380, MarketBeat consensus ~$412.51) still sits ~41–45% above the price. Ex-dividend Dec 7, 2026.

## Macro watch

**The week the jobs report made the December hike the base case — and the midterms made the AI buildout a political question.** The October jobs report (+165k vs ~65k expected, unemployment 4.1%, wages +0.4% m/m) lifted December hike odds to ~75–80% (CME FedWatch ~76–77%; Reuters ~80%; LSEG 75%) and the 10-year to ~4.88–4.89%. The University of Michigan's preliminary November sentiment actually improved — 51 vs 48 in October — and one-year inflation expectations eased to 4.3% from 4.7%. The economy is not rolling over; that is precisely why the Fed has room to hike. The ISM prints backed it up: manufacturing 55.5 (three-month high), services 54.5 (28th straight month of expansion).

The political leg is the new one. Tuesday's midterm sweep put Democrats in charge of both chambers, and the market spent the rest of the week pricing the consequences for data-centre construction — permits, power, antitrust scrutiny all back on the table. The Texas governor-elect pledged a moratorium; voters in Ohio, Illinois, Maine and Florida passed local bans or restrictions. UBS estimates nearly one-third of the computing power projected to come online in the US in 2028 is now at risk. The upside the weekend press notes: a slower buildout forces the hyperscalers to curb spending — the same spending investors punished after the recent earnings prints.

- **NVDA — hold into Nov 17.** The print decides the whole complex, and the options market prices a ~±10.9% move. Below the 200-day with RSI ~31, the chart is oversold but not basing; the levels are $181–185 below and $195–200 above. Nothing to do before Tuesday the 17th except watch the tape into it.
- **TSM — wait for confirmation or a $400 reclaim.** The 3D DRAM report is the most interesting new variable in the group — a foundry entering memory at its biggest customer's request would be a structural shift — but it is unconfirmed, with no timeline or capex. Below the 200-day at $375.44, the chart says wait.
- **ASML — watch, don't chase.** Deepest drawdown in the group (−9.01% on the week), furthest below its 200-day, RSI 34.6. The consensus target implies ~50% upside, but the order book is the question and no order has been cut — the de-rating is policy, not demand. A close back above $1,450 is the first repair signal.
- **META — hold.** $600 held twice this week, and the stock is the cheapest mega-cap on forward earnings (~18.5x) against the highest consensus upside in the group (~39%). The offset is the funding stack: equity, preferred, and now a reported $20–25B bond issue, plus insider selling across the top of the house. The Q4 guide ($73–76B) is the next fundamental test.
- **AMZN — the strongest watchlist chart; hold the position.** Above both moving averages, $250 reclaimed, week's winner at +7.88%. The next test is the Q4 guide midpoint ($31B operating income vs ~$33B expected) — and a close back below $250 would undo the repair. The AWS $1-trillion ambition (5–10 years) is the long frame; the negative trailing free cash flow (−$46.89B) is the near-term constraint.
- **AAPL (possible add) — no add this week.** The consensus target ($339) still sits below the price, the margin guide is the debate, and the add case needs either memory-cost pressure easing or a December-quarter beat — neither is visible yet. The position is the most resilient in the group; let the December quarter prove the margin story before adding.

**The one-line summary:** the week split the group into cash-flow names and buildout names, and the market paid the first group and punished the second. Tuesday's CPI and the 17th's Nvidia print are the two events that decide whether Friday's bounce was a bottom or a pause.
