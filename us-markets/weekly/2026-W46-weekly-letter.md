## The week in review

The week Nvidia lost its founder — and the market found somewhere else to put the money.

Monday was repair, and it looked like a turning point. After last week's washout (S&P −2.7%, Nasdaq −3.4%), the tape bounced hard: the S&P ran ~+2.4% and the Nasdaq-100 ~+3.4%, with chips leading (SOXX +5.7% at midday). Every name in the universe rose except one. **META +5.12%** and **AMZN +2.94%** on JPMorgan adding both to its Analyst Focus List — Meta replacing Alphabet, with Doug Anmuth arguing Meta's ad engine is now visibly out-earning Google Search (Q3 ad revenue +26% to $63.1B; JPMorgan expects Family of Apps ad revenue to pass Google Search from Q4). **ASML +6.21%** and **TSM +3.51%** rode the chip bounce. And **NVDA −4.28%**, the only big chip name deep in the red, on its own governance story: the Financial Times report that US intelligence had drafted a memo about CEO Jensen Huang, the board probe, and House chairs demanding a closed-door briefing on November 18. Volume ran ~2.4x average. AMD rose 11% the same day. The split was the story before the story.

Tuesday was the macro day, and it cut both ways. October CPI printed hot — headline +0.4% month over month (vs +0.3% expected), core +0.3% for the third straight month — hours after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in London that a core print of 0.3% or more meant a 50bp December hike "should be considered" and that hiking only 25bp would put the Fed's "credibility at risk." The market fell, but modestly: S&P ~−0.4%, Nasdaq-100 ~−0.7%. The real damage was concentrated in one name again: **NVDA −5.44%**, its second straight day of heavy selling, to $168.95 — ~30% below the October 6 record, its lowest close since late March, sitting just above the 52-week low of $164.27. The headlines compounded through the day: Bloomberg reported the FBI is investigating; the White House is weighing new export controls; the Rosen Law Firm filed a shareholder class action. Microsoft passed Nvidia in market value for the first time since June 2025. The two green names in the universe told the other half of the story: **TSM +2.04%** on October revenue of NT$586.89B, up 59.7% year over year — the biggest month ever — and **AMZN +1.46%**, still riding the JPMorgan catalyst.

Wednesday was the split, priced violently. CoreWeave reported after Tuesday's bell — Q3 revenue $3.66B, +168% year over year, backlog a record $132.5B, full-year guidance raised — and the AI supply chain roared back: S&P +1.40%, Nasdaq Composite +2.06%, SOX ~+3%. But the tape split the universe in two. **TSM +4.25%** was the day's best name, on the CoreWeave rally plus its own board meeting: up to $500M each into Anthropic (at the IPO price) and OpenAI preferred stock, $40.93B of capital appropriations, and the Q3 dividend raised to NT$9 from NT$7. **NVDA +2.87%** snapped the slide, though it faded from a +5% premarket and a $177.54 intraday high — and the analyst tape split with it: Raymond James upgraded to Strong Buy; Baird cut to Neutral and more than halved its target, $500 → $200. **AAPL +2.04%** closed at a new 52-week high ($355.63) on a China Renaissance upgrade to Buy, $425. **GOOGL +2.11%** on the Anthropic S-1: Google will invest another $10B at the IPO price, lifting its stake to ~13.7% of Anthropic, worth ~$132.4B at the $74 midpoint. And then the other side: **MSFT −2.26%**, **META −1.97%**, **AMZN −1.74%** — all falling while the market rose. The wedge was Anthropic's Claude Sheets, Whiteboards and Data launch — a spreadsheet tool aimed squarely at the $27.8B Microsoft 365 business — reviving the February "SaaSpocalypse" software scare. Meta's infrastructure chief Santosh Janardhan announced he will step down in early 2027. Silicon took the money; software gave it back.

Thursday was the day the founder premium came off. Nvidia announced before the open that **Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately**, ending his 33-year run. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO (keeping the CFO role); board member Harvey C. Jones becomes Nvidia's first-ever board chair. The stock opened −13% at $151.01, printed a new 52-week low of $148.80, then clawed back $17 off the low to close −4.34% at $166.19 on ~642M shares — ~4.4x average volume, the heaviest of the year. Seven brokerages downgraded between Wednesday and Thursday morning and targets were roughly halved: Bernstein $400 → $200 ("we've had enough"), Evercore $465 → $186, Melius $315 → $185. The rest of the tape mostly refused to follow it down: **GOOGL +1.79%** was the rotation's landing spot for the second day running, **MSFT +0.36%** stabilised, **AAPL −0.22%** touched a new intraday all-time high ($359.37) on its ex-dividend date. The chip supply chain took collateral damage — **TSM −1.55%**, **ASML −2.43%** — while the money that left Nvidia went to AMD and Broadcom (+~5% each). The 10-year Treasury jumped 12bp to 5.11%.

Friday was the give-back, and the week's lesson in one session. The S&P fell 0.75%, the Nasdaq 1.24%, led by chips — and this time the succession trade reversed with it: AMD gave back its entire Thursday rally (−8.8%), Broadcom fell 4.8%. Even good news didn't help: Cisco and Applied Materials both beat and raised guidance after Thursday's close, and both fell Friday (−6.2% and −2.7%). October PPI came in a touch softer than expected (+0.3% vs +0.4%), which trimmed 50bp December-hike odds — but the 10-year still closed at 5.13%, up ~27–30bp on the week. **NVDA −2.01%** was follow-through selling with no fresh company news, three more target cuts (Barclays $275 → $210, Truist to Hold at $174, HSBC to Hold at $170), its worst week in over a year and, per Reuters, its longest weekly losing streak since October 2012. **TSM −2.02%** closed below $400, the level it had defended all week. The rotation split held to the end: **META +2.90%** was the day's winner on no news at all, and **AAPL +0.40%** printed another intraday all-time high ($360.83).

For the week, the group split exactly along the line drawn two weeks ago — cash-flow and rotation names up, the AI epicentre down. **TSM +6.20%** was the week's winner despite Friday; **META +5.17%**, **ASML +4.94%**, **AAPL +4.06%**, **GOOGL +3.86%** and **AMZN +2.83%** all gained; **MSFT −1.27%** was near flat; **NVDA −12.75%** lost ~$470B of market value in five sessions and now sits ~33% below its October 6 high, −12.7% year to date, with a market cap of ~$3.9T — below $4T. The indices still ended green (S&P +2.15%, Nasdaq +1.92%, Dow +1.83%), which is the week's real tell: the market absorbed the biggest single-name governance shock of the year and still closed the week up, because the money didn't leave equities — it moved inside them.

## Macro watch

**The week the December hike became a near-certainty — and the 50bp question stayed alive.** October CPI printed hot on Tuesday (headline +0.4% month over month vs +0.3% expected, core +0.3% for the third straight month; no year-over-year figure — October 2025 prices were never collected during the 2025 shutdown, so the BLS publishes month-over-month only), hours after Governor Waller said a 50bp December hike "should be considered" if core printed 0.3% or higher. Chicago Fed President Goolsbee called the report "borderline disturbing." October PPI on Friday was a touch softer (+0.3% vs +0.4% expected; +6.5% year over year, the highest since November 2022), which trimmed 50bp odds — but the 10-year still closed the week at ~5.13–5.15%, up ~27–30bp on the week. The market now prices a December hike of some size as a near-certainty (~100%); only the size is debated — central estimate **~89% for 25bp, ~11% for 50bp** (CME FedWatch, Nov 13–14; the range across sources is ~86–94% for 25bp, intraday repricing rather than disagreement). A hike repricing lifts the discount rate on long-duration growth cash flows and raises the hurdle for AI capex funded at the margin — the direct channel for every name in this letter. With the 10-year above 5%, the equity risk premium is thin: the S&P 500 forward P/E was 17.7x on November 5, an earnings yield of ~5.65% against a 5.13% 10-year — a ~52bp premium, the lowest cushion in years. The offset, for this universe: the same inflation print reflects resilient nominal demand, and NVDA/TSM/ASML earnings are driven more by AI capex orders than by rates in the near term. The December 9 SEP and dot plot — not just the 25 vs 50bp call — is what will reset the 2027 path for these names.
