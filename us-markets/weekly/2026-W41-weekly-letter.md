## The week in review

The week the AI trade stopped moving as a basket.

It began with records. On Monday the Nasdaq printed its third record in two weeks, NVDA closed at a fresh all-time high of $238.90, and TSM rode "Terafab" chatter to its own intraday record. On Tuesday, NVDA set the mark again — **$239.24 close, $243.37 intraday** — as BNP Paribas raised its target to $345 and Google's 890-megawatt nuclear PPA with Constellation carried utilities and the S&P 500 to its first record since August. A 10-year yield at a 24-year high (5.364% Wednesday) and Brent over $100 didn't stop the bid.

Then Thursday arrived with the week's two shocks. The **FT reported OpenAI's annualized revenue is running ~$50B, roughly $20B below the ~$70B previously signaled to investors** — a number the whole AI-capex complex had been priced against. Chips fell 2–7% across the board (SOX −3.4%, its steepest since mid-August): NVDA −2.94%, TSM −3.00%, ASML −1.90%. OpenAI later pushed back — it still expects to reach or exceed $70B by year-end, and CNN noted the gap is largely a net-vs-gross accounting artifact (OpenAI counts net revenue excluding cloud-partner sales) — but the repricing had happened. The second shock was oil: **Brent settled +4.1% at $104.28** on escalating Strait of Hormuz tanker attacks plus a Gulf hurricane shut-in, before President Trump's Truth Social post — no Iran attack before the November 3 midterms, "productive discussions" ongoing — eased crude on Friday.

Friday was the relief rebound, but selective: Dow +0.8%, S&P 500 +0.6% (just off its record), Nasdaq +0.6% — the Nasdaq's fourth straight weekly gain. For the week: **S&P +1.1%, Dow +0.9%, Nasdaq +0.6%**. The tell of the week was in our own eight names: NVDA and TSM both opened up Friday and closed red — the AI complex is now being marked name-by-name, not bought as a basket.

**AMZN (+4.3%)** was the universe's leader on positioning into October 29, **MSFT (+3.4%)** rode an analyst-day cluster and a Windows event, and **GOOGL (+2.4%)** quietly built the week's best relative strength. **ASML (−4.7%)** and **TSM (−4.1%)** were the laggards — de-risking into Wednesday and Thursday's earnings, not demand breaks: TSM's September revenue was +54.6% YoY, and its Q3 beat its own guidance ceiling. **AAPL (+0.9%)** decoupled on product-event positioning until a Friday Nikkei order-cut report halved its weekly gain. **META (−1.3%)** lagged every leg of the week's round trip despite a confirmed golden cross and the Street's highest target jumping to $1,000.

## Fundamentals

### NVDA — Nvidia · $229.28 · P/E ~29.7 (ttm) / ~16.5x (fwd, late-Sep LSEG) · consensus ~$322 (+40%)

The stock of the week, twice over. Tuesday's **$239.24 record close** (intraday $243.37) had the $6T milestone ~4% away and the forward multiple near historic lows (~16.5x, "the lowest since January 2015" per the LSEG-sourced reporting — flag: a late-September print, not this week). Wednesday's FT exclusive — **SpaceX in talks to raise ~$40B ($10B loans + $30B investment-grade debt, closing expected 2027) to buy Nvidia chips**, Apollo leading — read both ways: insatiable demand, and ever more leverage stacked into the AI capex complex.

Thursday was the week's defining single headline for the whole trade: the FT's OpenAI revenue report. NVDA fell **−2.94%** (~$169B of market cap), with Micron, Broadcom, Intel, Oracle, and CoreWeave all down 4–8%. The counter-narrative (CNN): it's an accounting gap, not a demand shortfall. Friday opened +1.5% and faded to −0.52% — dip-buying in the morning, selling in the stock into the close, with Barron's-cited options flow showing buyers of Oct-23 $240 calls into **GTC Berlin (Oct 20–22, Huang keynotes Oct 21)**.

The week's other genuinely new item: **Huang appeared on stage with Nadella at Microsoft's Windows/Surface event to unveil RTX Spark AI PCs** — Blackwell GPU + Grace CPU, up to 128GB unified memory, ~1 petaflop of local AI performance, Surface Laptop Ultra at $2,599 shipping October 16. Nvidia is opening a PC socket it has never owned: a real long-term expansion, a 2027-scale ramp rather than a 2026 earnings driver. Also this week: the unsealed *Groq* class action alleges the ~$20B Dec-2025 Nvidia licensing arrangement was a de facto sale without a shareholder vote — **Nvidia is not named as a defendant**.

**Street this week:** BNP Paribas' Karl Ackerman raised **$285 → $345** (Outperform, Oct 5): agentic AI needs more hardware, the CUDA moat sustains 70%+ gross margins; Morgan Stanley reinstated NVDA as its top semiconductor pick (**$300**, Oct 2); UBS reaffirmed Buy $300 (Oct 9, secondhand). Earnings date still unconfirmed (Zacks: Nov 18; TipRanks: Nov 25). Consensus for Q3 FY27: **EPS ~$2.47 / revenue ~$109B**, against the company's own **$108B ±2% guide** (no China data-center revenue assumed).

### GOOGL — Alphabet · $351.66 · P/E ~17.6 (ttm) / ~26.2 (fwd) · consensus ~$426 (+21%)

The week's best relative-strength name among the hyperscalers, and the week rewarded long-duration power contracts as an AI moat. Tuesday's verified package with **Constellation: a 20-year PPA for 890 MW of new nuclear** (uprates at 11 units across IL/PA/NJ, first online 2028, Constellation investing $4.3B+) **plus a 15-year, 2,700-MW deal** from the existing fleet and a 5-year Google Cloud/Gemini Enterprise tie-up. On the risk side: Reuters carried a letter from **120+ US lawmakers over Google's $10M bankruptcy-auction win of Spirit Airlines' internal data** for AI training (Google says it won't seek personal info; a bankruptcy hearing Oct 14 decides), and Finland's regulator halted work at two Google data-center sites pending environmental review (minor; part of the €13B Finland buildout).

**Street this week:** TD Cowen raised **$475 → $485** (Buy, Oct 8); Wells Fargo raised **$411 → $417** (Oct 6, arguing TPU-driven 2027 revenue sits 14% above Street); Needham reiterated **Buy $450** (Oct 8). ## Macro watch

**The minutes week.** Wednesday's FOMC minutes (September 15–16) confirmed the 12–0 vote to hike 25 bp to **3.75–4.00%** — the first hike in three years — and that **"most participants" see another increase appropriate by year-end**. The tape read it as priced in: October pause odds held around **~81–83%** (they have slipped from ~93% — verify live FedWatch before the event), with the debate now centered on **December** (hike odds ~60–86% depending on source). **The next FOMC meeting is October 27–28, decision 2 PM ET Wednesday October 28** — with a press conference. (A small correction to the daily briefs, which said October 28–29; federalreserve.gov says 27–28.)

**The speaker tape was hawkish with an AI twist.** Gov. Waller (Oct 8, Istanbul): additional hikes "likely needed," but October pause possible — "hikes do not need to come at consecutive meetings." Gov. Barr (Sep 29): AI buildout has "knocked [the Fed] off course" on 2%; Gov. Kashkari (Oct 1): neutral rate risen to 3.25%, partly AI-driven; Vice Chair Jefferson (Oct 1): the decision "may take more time." **Chair Warsh's first big signal moment comes Friday, October 16** — a fireside chat with IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva in Bangkok.

**The inflation gauntlet is this Wednesday.** September **CPI lands 8:30 AM ET October 14** alongside the Beige Book — consensus **3.6–3.7% headline / 2.4–2.5% core** (August: 3.4%/2.4%; gasoline +27.4% y/y drove it). A hot core print re-prices toward an October hike; a cool one locks in the pause — **six hours before ASML reports**. September PPI + retail sales land Thursday (same morning as TSM), with Q3 GDP advance October 29 and September PCE also October 29 (market-reported; not primary-verified).

**Yields and oil.** The 10-year tagged **5.364% Wednesday** (24-year high; 30-year 5.732%) before strong auctions pulled it back to **~5.24–5.26%** into Friday. **Brent settled +4.1% at $104.28** Thursday on Hormuz tanker attacks and a Gulf hurricane shut-in, eased Friday after Trump's no-strike-before-midterms post, but held above $100. **UMich preliminary October sentiment was 46.3** (vs 47.8 forecast) — current conditions at an all-time low (44.7), 1-year inflation expectations at 4.7%: a stagflationary mix the market chose to look through this week.

**What it means for these names.** The AI trade is simultaneously the market's shield and the rate problem's source: megacap tech outran a 24-year yield high, yet the Fed's own governors now name AI capex as an inflation driver — the market is pricing demand it believes in against a policy path designed to cool exactly that demand. The October 14–15 window is the crucible: CPI, ASML, and TSM in 36 hours, with the semis' record chatter squarely inside the debate about whether the AI capex boom has become a monetary-policy variable.
