## The week in review

The week the AI-spend bill came due — twice, in public, on consecutive nights.

Monday was repair, and it looked like a turning point. Anthropic's SEC S-1 filing and **META's ~$35B concurrent offerings** ($22.5B Class A via forward sales plus ~$12.5B mandatory convertible preferred, ticker to be METAM, plus a planned up-to-$60B Class C at-the-market program from Q1 2027) revived the AI-demand trade. Chips led: **NVDA +2.48%**, breaking the $215 resistance flagged all last week; **TSM +1.43%**, **ASML +1.12%**. META itself fell 2.15% — dilution first, partnership headlines second — and **GOOGL fell a seventh straight day**, closing at $316.94, below $320.

Tuesday was the chip chain's third straight gain and META's continued slide. **TSM +2.89%, ASML +2.77%** (rising through its ex-dividend date, EUR 1.88, payable November 4), **AMZN +1.71%** into Thursday's print, **GOOGL +1.49%** — its first green day after seven down days, back above $320. META fell another 1.60% to $620.49 despite the prospectus naming NVDA, AMD and Broadcom as cornerstone investors indicating interest in up to $5B combined of the offering (reports put NVDA's slice at ~$3B; the interest is non-binding).

Wednesday was the crucible: the **FOMC held the funds rate at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote** (Hammack and Cook dissenting in favour of a hike), with no December signal — and **GOOGL and MSFT reported after the close**. The tape itself was flat and wait-and-see (S&P +~0.2%, Nasdaq ~flat). **ASML +1.49%** made it three straight flagged days on the SK Hynix record result; **GOOGL +1.22%** on a TradingView report that OpenAI intends to buy Google's AI chips; **NVDA −1.37%**, fading from a $219.99 high into the Fed/earnings overhang; **META −1.34%**, below $615. Apple's "Pro and tell" event unveiled the touchscreen MacBook Pro Bloomberg had flagged — and **AAPL fell 0.94%**, a sell-the-news session.

Thursday was the reckoning, part one. **GOOGL's Q3 missed on both lines** — revenue $127.58B (+25% y/y) vs ~$128.2B consensus, the first revenue miss since Q4 2022; EPS $2.12 vs ~$3.02 consensus — and the stock fell **12.6% in its worst day since going public in 2004**, wiping out roughly $500B of market value on ~103M shares vs a ~21–31M average. **MSFT, by contrast, printed a genuine beat** — Q1 FY27 revenue $92.3B (+19%), adjusted EPS $4.89 vs $4.71, Azure +48% to $33.2B, free cash flow +$18.6B, Q2 Azure guided +49–50% constant currency — opened up 4–5% and faded to roughly flat. The fade was the tell: even a clean AI-spend beat with positive cash flow got no lasting premium, because GOOGL had reframed the whole complex. **AMZN −3.9%** ahead of its own print, on GOOGL sympathy plus hedging.

Friday was the reckoning, part two — and the worst day for the tape since June. **AMZN's Q3 missed**: revenue $201.94B vs ~$202.2B consensus, EPS $1.89 vs ~$1.97, even as AWS grew 39.3% to $45.98B; Q3 capex was $77.3B, 2026 capex held at ~$220B with 2027 guided "meaningfully" higher, and trailing free cash flow was **−$42.4B**. The stock fell ~7% after hours Thursday to ~$226, then V-recovered Friday off a $229.36 low to close at $240.08 (−1.8% on the day). **Intel's Q4 guide miss** (revenue $15–16B, adjusted EPS $0.25 vs ~$17.1B / $0.43 expected, PC weakness lasting at least into H1 2027) sent INTC down ~29–31% — its worst day since 1974 — and dragged the SOX down 9.15%. A Bloomberg report that **NVDA is in talks to guarantee billions in debt for an Nscale data centre Anthropic plans to use** (West Virginia campus; nothing signed) added a financing-contagion headline to the chip rout. **GOOGL fell a further 1.8%** to $278.80, a modest follow-through after Thursday's capitulation. **AAPL −1.3% was the most resilient name in the universe into its own Monday earnings.**

For the week, every name in the universe fell. **GOOGL −13.1%** was the worst week of any holding this quarter; **NVDA −4.5%** and **META −4.4%** followed; **AMZN −3.2%**, **TSM −2.7%**, **AAPL −1.0%**, **MSFT −0.8%**, **ASML −0.4%**. The October month-to-date picture from Morningstar, printed in the Friday recap, is the context: INTC −43.5%, GOOGL −18.5%, META −14.7%, NVDA −8.7%, AMZN −6.9%, AAPL +4.2%, MSFT +1.8%. The week's thesis, in one line: **the market stopped asking whether AI spending is real and started asking whether it is paid for — and it now grades every hyperscaler on free cash flow, with MSFT's +$18.6B the only passing mark among the three that reported.**

## Fundamentals

### GOOGL — Alphabet · $278.80 · P/E ~14.5 (Finnhub; TipRanks: 14.6, cheapest since 2012) · consensus ~$401–411 post-cuts

The print: **revenue $127.58B (+25% y/y) vs ~$128.2B consensus; EPS $2.12 vs ~$3.02** — but the gap is mostly non-operating: an ~$8.7B unrealised loss on equity securities (Motley Fool identifies the SpaceX stake) plus a $5.2B cash EU antitrust fine payment. The two real issues the market sold: **(1) Search revenue growth slowed to ~12%** (to $63.4B, vs ~$64.9B expected) from ~17% last quarter, which management blamed on currency, softer Asian retail ads and lower monetisation in AI Mode — the fear is AI search earns less per query than classic search; **(2) cash: Q3 capex $51.4B, free cash flow −$9.07B** (vs ~−$3.5B expected), 2026 capex guidance $195–205B kept, and 2027 spend guided to rise "significantly" (some analysts model ~$300B). Alphabet funded the build with $12.4B of stock sales and $28.7B of new debt in the quarter.

## Macro watch

**The week the Fed held and the market still priced a December hike.** The October 27–28 FOMC **held at 3.75–4.00%, 10–2**, with Hammack and Cook dissenting in favour of a hike and no December signal in the statement (Barron's live coverage; XTB analysis). Market pricing into the meeting had been ~90% for a December hike (per the week's briefs) — the hold with two hawkish dissents keeps December live without committing to it. The September minutes' warning — that the AI buildout could push demand above supply and raise inflation — now reads as the meeting's subtext: the week's oil shock (Brent ~$112–116 on Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia) is exactly the pass-through channel the hawks named, and the 10-year stuck near 5.0–5.1% with no safe-haven bid is the market charging duration for it.

**What it means for these names.** The AI trade is simultaneously the market's engine and the rate problem's source, and this week the market started charging the builders for it in cash terms: GOOGL's multiple compressed on a search-monetisation question plus negative free cash flow; AMZN's on a $220B capex bill rising "meaningfully" further; META's on dilution to fund the same bill. Monday's AAPL print is the referendum on the other side of the trade: the one mega-cap without an AI-capex problem, reporting into a tape that just punished every name that has one.
