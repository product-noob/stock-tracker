## The week in review

The week the AI trade got a credit check.

It started with rotation, not fear. Monday, the Dow rose while tech sagged on oil near $106 and rate-hike bets into Wednesday's CPI. **META's +3.36%** was the day's conviction trade — the Muse narrative repricing (Truist's $1,000, Pivotal's $930, Citi modeling $39B+ of Muse revenue by 2030) — and **AAPL's +1.14%** carried positioning into Tuesday's "Welcome Home" smart-home event. Tuesday delivered the event (HomeView at $299, HomePod mini 2, a new Apple TV 4K) and a **fresh AAPL 52-week high of $348.15** intraday — then the stock faded to flat, and the semis kept bleeding on China's CXMT memory claims. Waller, noting the yield surge was doing some of the Fed's work, kept the tape calm.

Then Wednesday brought the week's first shock. **September CPI printed hot — 3.7% y/y headline (vs 3.6% consensus), 2.6% core (vs 2.5%)** — and the Nasdaq fell 2.0% with the S&P 500 down 1.3%. Into that, **ASML reported genuinely strong numbers — record €11.66B sales, net income €4.96B (a clean beat), GM 57.5% (a beat), the FY26 guide raised a third time to €44–45B, and a warning that 2027–28 planned supply won't meet demand** — and got sold 3.2% anyway. Goldman cut META's target $860→$762 on 2027 opex fears (META −3.25%), CoreWeave fell 16%, and the neocloud-debt question moved from the sidelines to the center of the tape. The one green name: **AAPL (+0.76%), closing above a $5 trillion market cap for the first time ever** — $342.96, ~$5.005T — only the second company in history after Nvidia.

Thursday was the second beat-and-sold. **TSM printed the best quarter in its history — NT$763.1B net profit (+68.7% y/y, a beat), Q4 revenue guided to $52.3–54.3B, FY26 growth raised to ~44%** — and sold off 2.35% anyway, with a day low of $407.13 that held above $400. The macro didn't help: **September PPI ran +1.3% m/m (vs 0.5% expected, the biggest since March 2022)**, and retail sales doubled estimates at +0.8%. The tape responded with a defensive rotation — Dow +1.08%, Nasdaq flat — while MSFT (+1.25%) and GOOGL (+0.62%, JPMorgan raising its target $420→$425) acted like the AI trade's creditors, not its debtors. BofA cut its META Q3 EPS estimate nearly in half on a ~$10B one-time legal accrual, and the Street's MSFT target cluster marched to $600–675.

Friday was the credit day. CNN's close recap said it plainly: *"Nasdaq sinks more than 2% as investors raise questions about AI debt."* **CoreWeave fell another 12% — bond yields above 15%, down 60%+ since its March IPO peak** — and the market started pricing financed-buildout names like credit trades, not equity trades. The PHLX Semiconductor index fell for an **8th straight day, its longest losing streak since 2008**, down ~12% since the start of October. The **Nasdaq Composite closed −2.15% at 26,140.03, −4.48% on the week — its worst since June; the S&P 500 closed −1.09% at 7,659.44, −1.95% on the week; the Dow slipped just 0.34% to 52,002.41 and was +0.67% on the week.** The VIX surged ~30% to near 20. Oil kept the inflation bid alive — WTI +3.8% to $101.71, Brent ~$110.

For the week, our eight names split exactly the way Friday's tape did: **AAPL (+2.0%) the lone weekly gainer** — iPhone Duo preorders opened Friday and sold out in minutes in China, and Berkshire's new CEO Greg Abel named Apple a concentrated long-term holding in his first annual letter. **MSFT (−1.1%) and META (−1.3%)** were the least-damaged losers; **TSM (−9.0%), ASML (−7.8%), and NVDA (−6.6%)** took the semiconductor pain; **AMZN (−4.7%) and GOOGL (−3.2%)** drifted with the mega-cap tape. The week's thesis, in one line: **two beats that got sold, one hot CPI, and a credit scare — and the market now splits AI names into self-funders (AAPL, NVDA) versus bond-dependent builders.**

## Macro watch

**The week inflation won the argument — and the Fed got the last word anyway.** Wednesday's **September CPI** — headline +0.6% m/m / **3.7% y/y** (vs 3.6% expected), core +0.3% m/m / **2.6% y/y** (vs 2.5%), with gasoline +9.1% m/m doing more than half the work — took the October hike from a ~22% tail to ~42–44% by Wednesday evening. Thursday's **September PPI** buried the tail further: **+1.3% m/m (vs 0.5% expected, the largest since March 2022), 6.3% y/y (biggest since December 2022), goods +2.6% — the largest since the series began.** September retail sales doubled estimates at **+0.8%**, though gasoline's +6.4% did the work and the control group was a soft +0.1%.

And then the week repriced it all back down. The **Warsh–Georgieva fireside (Bangkok, Friday local)** carried no forward guidance but the signal was clear: an **October pause with a "second and final hike in December"** — October-hike odds were below 15% by Friday, December the next live question. **The next FOMC meeting is October 27–28 (verified on federalreserve.gov); the decision statement lands 2:00 PM ET Wednesday, October 28 — with a Warsh press conference and no dot plot; the next SEP is December 8–9. The Fed is in blackout October 17–29.** The minutes of the September meeting added the week's most important sentence for these names: **"some [participants] said the artificial intelligence buildout could push demand above supply over the medium term and raise inflation"** — the Fed has now named the AI capex boom as a policy variable. Waller (Oct 13) put the number on it: hyperscalers are "planning on spending trillions of dollars over the next three years," keeping demand pressure up.

**Yields and oil.** The 10-year closed Friday at **5.09%, down 15bp on the week** — falling *despite* two hot inflation prints, as tech-selloff money rotated into duration. It topped 5.30% earlier in October (the highest since 2002). **Brent settled ~$110, WTI +3.8% Friday to $101.71** on Middle East supply fears — oil is now the active inflation risk into the FOMC.

**The credit lens.** Friday's market finally put a name on the week's fear: the AI-credit trade. **CoreWeave −12% Friday (−25% over five sessions, −60%+ since its March IPO peak), debt load past $51B, bond yields above 15%.** Morgan Stanley IM's Jim Caron: "at risk of a tech-led correction." ARK bought the dip (CoreWeave, Cerebras). The Anthropic IPO is reportedly targeting **$1.5–2T**. And the week split AI names in two, in tradingnews' framing: firms that **self-fund capex from operating cash** (Nvidia, Apple) versus firms that **depend on bond markets** (neoclouds) — "in a market with a 5.09% 10-year yield the second group is being treated as rate-sensitive."

**What it means for these names.** The AI trade is simultaneously the market's engine and the rate problem's source: the Fed's own governors now name AI capex as an inflation driver while the market reprices anything levered to data-center debt as a credit trade. The October 28 window is the crucible: **the FOMC decision at 2 PM ET, then MSFT and GOOGL earnings the same afternoon, AMZN on the 29th, and Sep PCE + Q3 GDP advance at 8:30 AM on the 29th (GDPNow 3.6% SAAR).** Duration risk for mega-cap growth is the thread running through all of it.
