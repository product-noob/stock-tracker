## The week in review

The week the jobs report rescued the AI trade — and nearly didn't.

The first four days belonged to the bond bears. The 10-year touched 5.34% on Thursday, its highest in 24 years, the 30-year traded around 5.59–5.62%, and equities wobbled: the S&P 500 fell three straight sessions to start the quarter while AI-capex financing dominated the worry list. Fed Vice Chair Jefferson urged patience after September's hike to 3.75–4.00%, but Kashkari and others pushed back hard, and CME odds of an October hike still sat near 64% a week earlier.

Then Friday's September employment report landed at 8:30 AM ET and rewrote the tape: **+29K payrolls** (vs ~84K Dow Jones consensus / ~90K Reuters), **unemployment 4.2%** (up from 4.1%), wage growth +0.1% m/m and just +3.0% y/y — the slowest annual gain since May 2021. Net revisions shaved another 60K off prior months. October hike odds collapsed to **~17–23%**. The 10-year dipped to 5.15% before rebounding to 5.28% into the close, and tech ripped: Nasdaq +1.2% to 27,190.86 (a new intraday record), S&P 500 +0.7% to 7,722.72 (~1% off its high), Dow +0.49% to 51,176.96.

For the full week the picture was mixed: the Nasdaq managed +0.5% (a third straight weekly gain) while the S&P 500 slipped 0.3% and the Dow 1.3%. Gold fell 3.3% to ~$4,172, WTI held ~$91 (Brent $102.72) with an OPEC+ meeting on Sunday, and the VIX ended at 15.31 — up 3% on the week, still more nervous than panicked.

In our universe, semis carried the week. **ASML (+7.1%)** and **TSM (+4.9%)** rode Micron's blowout (rev $54.23B, EPS $33.42, supply tight through FY27–28) and the post-jobs rate reprieve into Friday. **NVDA (+4.0%)** printed an intraday record $237.88 on Friday but — correction from our mid-week notes — did *not* set an all-time closing high: May 14's $235.74 still stands. **GOOGL (−0.1%)** ended the week essentially flat after the Gemini 4 Argon launch faded. **META (−3.1%)** was the week's worst performer as New Mexico asked a judge for $35–40B in the Cambridge Analytica case. **AAPL (−2.2%)** fell on valuation gravity despite strong iPhone 18 Pro launch data from China. **MSFT (+0.3%)** and **AMZN (+0.7%)** were steady.

## Fundamentals

### NVDA — Nvidia · $233.95 · P/E ~29.6 (ttm) / ~25.6 (fwd) · consensus ~$324 (+39%)

The loudest capital-allocation statement of the week anywhere: on September 28 the board authorized a **$150B buyback increase**, taking total remaining authorization to ~$235B — the largest repurchase program in corporate history, surpassing Apple's $110B from 2024. The message is that management views the stock as cheap at ~25–26x forward earnings, and it mechanically tightens the EPS path.

The week's other thread was the one bear narrative with legs: Reuters reported **lenders are balking at Nvidia's plan to raise up to $500B** for AI-infrastructure expansion, questioning whether chips hold enough collateral value, and FT coverage of Nvidia's early talks with insurers about underwriting collateral risk on "neocloud" loans kept the circular-financing debate alive. It didn't touch the tape — Micron's memory-super-cycle message and the jobs report did the lifting instead.

**Street this week:** Morgan Stanley's Joseph Moore reinstated NVDA as his top semiconductor pick on Friday (Overweight, **$300 target**); Rosenblatt's Buy and $390 — the Street high — stood. Consensus implies ~+39% upside.

### GOOGL — Alphabet · $343.50 · P/E ~17.3 (ttm) / ~25.6 (fwd) · consensus ~$425–426 (+24%)

The defining product moment: Tuesday's **Gemini 4 Argon** launch — flagship frontier model, intro pricing $2/$10 per million tokens (roughly a fifth of the rival intro rates), beating the competition on DeepSWE and tying on the Artificial Analysis Index at ~60% lower task cost. Citi said the launch removes a "key overhang"; Jefferies' Brad Thill argued the ~50% cheaper pricing could force rival cuts — "a game of leapfrog." The stock's verdict: popped on launch day (+0.93%), then the classic sell-the-launch fade on Thursday (−1.70%, an intraday high of $353.22), then a +1.56% jobs-day lift on Friday. The week finished −0.12%.

Elsewhere: Google **challenged two EU DMA orders** at the Luxembourg General Court (search-data sharing, Gemini access for rivals) and asked the court to suspend the search-data order; California's SB 1246 robotaxi law was signed (Waymo at the center). **Street:** Piper Sandler raised $395 → $400 (Overweight) on Monday; BNP Paribas maintained Buy ($420) post-Argon.

### AAPL — Apple (holding + watchlist) · $333.69 · P/E ~37.4 (ttm) / ~35–38 (fwd) · consensus ~$340 (+2%)

The paradox stock of the week. Counterpoint data showed **iPhone 18 Pro China launch-week sales +12% YoY** — ~1.3M units in the first week, 33% of China weekly smartphone sales and #1 — while the overall China market fell 17%. Apple's deliberate skip of the standard iPhone 18 launch is funneling upgrades toward pricier Pros. The stock fell anyway: −2.16% on the week.

Why? Valuation, mostly: at ~35–38x forward — a premium to MSFT (~27x) and GOOGL (~26x) — the consensus target (~$340) sits essentially *at* the price. There is no room for "fine." Morgan Stanley **cut its target $360 → $355** (kept Overweight) on October 1: stronger builds lift revenue, but lower iPhone ASPs and higher memory/component costs leave earnings little changed. Bernstein echoed the memory-cost concern as a Q1 FY27 gross-margin risk. Also on the tape: the **foldable iPhone Duo** (pre-order Oct 16, retail Oct 23, $1,999 — CNBC, Oct 1), a September 25 jury's **record $5.72B Taptic Engine patent verdict** for Taction Technology (not willful; Apple will appeal), and Bloomberg reporting new CEO John Ternus is weighing an engineering-focused restructuring.

**Catalysts ahead:** the **October 13 smart-home event** (home hub, HomePod mini, Apple TV, deeper Siri AI, per Gurman), the **Duo foldable launch**, and **Q4 FY26 earnings ~October 29** (consensus ~$2.02 EPS / ~$115.06B rev; Apple's own guide: revenue +9–11%, gross margin 47–48%) — the decisive readout on iPhone 18 demand and whether memory costs bite margins.

### MSFT — Microsoft · $517.53 · P/E ~28.5 (ttm) / ~26.7 (fwd) · consensus ~$571 (+10%)

A quiet, constructive week. **Wells Fargo raised its target $700 → $725** (Overweight) — the Street high — added Microsoft to its Q4 Tactical Ideas list, and cited AI monetization plus a "bigger than usual" November 17–20 Ignite; **Piper Sandler raised $550 → $610** (Overweight) on September 30, quantifying a real lever: a 10% shift of M365 seats from E5 to E7 is worth ~$2B annualized revenue. Paid **Copilot seats surpassed 30 million**. The company will report under two segments — Agents and Infra; Devices and Consumer — starting with fiscal Q1, reorganizing its financial storytelling around the agentic era. No sell-side drama: 43 Buy / 4 Hold / 1 Sell.

Context: the stock consolidated a ~39% September quarter, and earnings land **~October 27–28** (unconfirmed) — Azure growth is the central measure, with Azure dollar-revenue disclosure starting this quarter.

### META — Meta Platforms · $728.08 · P/E ~27 (ttm) · consensus ~$788 (+8%)

Meta was the week's worst performer (−3.1%) for a reason that isn't going away soon. At a post-trial hearing on October 1, **New Mexico asked Judge Francis Mathew to impose $35–40B** after the September 25 jury verdict found the company misled consumers about Facebook data privacy (the Cambridge Analytica case; 43.9M violations of the state's Unfair Practices Act at $5,000 each implies a ~$219B theoretical maximum). Meta called the ask "astronomical" and wants a $3.45B cap; the judge postponed, with a decision due later in October. Even a negotiated-down order is a major overhang event — and it caps how far the multiple can run while unresolved.

Underneath, the setup remains strong: the Muse personal AI agent drew ~5M downloads (Sep 8–30, Sensor Tower), Zuckerberg's Connect thesis — Muse free "for a huge number of tokens," profit via transaction fees, not ads — is intact, and the >$130B AI investment program rolled on (a reported $27B Nebius capacity agreement). Needham reiterated Hold on October 1: Meta "clearly pivoted… toward personal Agentic AI," but is "notoriously slow at monetizing new products." The stock sits ~8% below its $790 record and ~17–18% above its 50-day moving average — an uptrend interrupted by a legal headline, not a fundamental one.

### TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor (ADR) · $472.78 · P/E ~29.2 (ttm) / ~20.1 (fwd) · consensus ~$552 (+17%)

The foundry that underwrites everyone else's AI ambitions had the week's second-best showing (+4.9%). Bloomberg reported TSMC is weighing a **multibillion-dollar Texas campus near Dallas** — multiple fabs at ~$20B each — on top of the $265B Arizona commitment, as AI-driven demand strains capacity; early stage, but the second hub is a question of when, not if. Micron's blowout lifted the whole semi complex, and Friday's Broadcom–Anthropic financing news ($60B bank debt package reported by Bloomberg) added a bid. Q3 earnings land **October 15** (Zacks consensus ~$4.39 EPS, ~$45.8B revenue — the top of the company's own $44.6–45.8B guide); September monthly revenue (~October 9–10) is the appetizer. August revenue was NT$514.81B, +53.3% YoY — the first NT$500B+ month.

### ASML — ASML Holding · $1,867.31 · P/E ~58.9 (ttm) / ~33.7 (fwd) · consensus ~$2,095 (+12%)

The week's best performer (+7.1%) on a Wall Street reiteration cluster ahead of **Q3 earnings October 14** (pre-market): **UBS reiterated Buy (€2,350 target)** on September 28, naming ASML its preferred EU semi pick and noting 2027–28 lithography capacity targets may grow above 30%; Jefferies reaffirmed Neutral on September 30. The technology story keeps compounding — Samsung joined the 12-inch High-NA EUV photomask initiative, planning High-NA EUV in DRAM volume manufacturing by 2028. No new export-control developments this week (standing overhang: the draft MATCH Act). At ~59x trailing, ASML remains the highest-multiple name in the group — you are paying for the monopoly on the machines that make the machines.

### AMZN — Amazon · $251.52 · P/E ~20 (ttm) · consensus ~$321–332 (+28–32%)

Amazon was the week's analyst darling and a steady performer (+0.7%). **Goldman Sachs added AMZN to its US Conviction List** (Buy, $375 target, ~+49% upside — thesis: multi-year revenue compounding, margin expansion, AI-compute demand, the embryonic ad platform; Goldman's EPS estimates sit 8% above consensus). **Rosenblatt raised $335 → $360** (Buy) and **New Street reiterated Top Pick, raised to $385** on AWS margin expansion. Fifty-six of 59 analysts are Buy or equivalent — the widest Street gap in the group.

The infrastructure buildout was tangible: **AWS signed a >$1B multiyear deal with Synopsys** for chip-design IP (Trainium/Graviton), and Amazon signed a **20-year PPA with Constellation for 690 MW of nuclear power** at Calvert Cliffs. Friday's spiciest item: the FT reported Amazon is **sounding investors on moving ~$8B of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips into an SPV and leasing them back** — exploratory, but a telling asset-light play against a ~$220B 2026 capex year. **Prime Big Deal Days (October 6–7)** is the near-term demand read; **Q3 earnings ~October 29** (consensus ~$2.01 EPS / ~$202.01B rev — the top of the company's own $197–202B guide) is the resolution.

## Macro watch

**The jobs report changed the Fed's calendar.** September payrolls +29K vs ~84–90K consensus, unemployment up to 4.2%, wages +3.0% y/y (slowest since May 2021), and net −60K in revisions — the hiring plateau is real. October hike odds collapsed from ~64% a week ago to **~17–23%**; the base case is now a hold on **October 27–28**, with the debate moved to December. The speaker tape is split: Jefferson (Thu) said the labor market has "stabilized" with layoffs low; Williams sees "no need for urgency" on another hike; Barr says further adjustments are "likely," explicitly citing AI spending pushing up chip prices; Kashkari (Thu) "doesn't know how high rates will have to go."

**Inflation.** September **CPI lands Wednesday, October 14** (8:30 AM ET) — the make-or-break input for the October 28 decision. Reference points: August printed 3.4% headline / 2.4% core. A hot core print re-prices toward a hike; a cool one locks in the hold. The Beige Book drops the same afternoon.

**The week ahead:** ISM Services (Mon Oct 5, consensus 55.6); **FOMC minutes** (Wed Oct 7, 2 PM ET — the September meeting's); jobless claims (Thu, consensus 205K); Michigan sentiment prelim (Fri, consensus 48.0). Later in the window: PPI + retail sales (Oct 15), **FOMC October 27–28** (decision 2 PM ET Oct 28), **Q3 GDP advance (Oct 28, same morning)** and September PCE (Oct 29). Fed blackout conventionally starts ~October 17. Note: a continuing resolution funds the federal government through December 11, so there is no data blackout — October releases proceed on schedule.

**What it means for these names.** The AI trade is simultaneously the market's shield and the rate problem's source: megacap tech outperformed the yield spike, yet AI capex is, in the Fed's own telling, adding to the demand for capital and raising borrowing costs. The October 14–15 window is the crucible — CPI and the Beige Book on Wednesday, then ASML and TSM earnings — with the semis' record chatter (NVDA's intraday high, Broadcom–Anthropic vendor financing) squarely inside the debate about whether the AI capex boom is now a monetary-policy variable.
