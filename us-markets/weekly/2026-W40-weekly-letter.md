## The week in review

The week the bond market finally lost patience with the AI boom.

Treasuries just printed their worst quarter since 1994. The 10-year touched 5.32–5.34% on Thursday — the highest since 2002 — and the 30-year traded around 5.64–5.66%. The ingredients: Middle East escalation (a third US carrier strike group reportedly heading to the region), Brent back above $100–102, fiscal anxiety, and — uncomfortably close to our watchlist — AI infrastructure capex draining the global capital pool. Fed Governor Barr said the quiet part out loud: AI spending is pushing up chip prices, and AI capex is "adding to the demand for capital, raising borrowing costs."

The one dovish thread came from inflation data with an asterisk. August PCE printed 3.4% headline / 3.0% core, well below consensus of 3.7%/3.3% — but the beat was largely mechanical, driven by a BEA methodology revision (software, legal, and portfolio pricing, retroactive to 2021) that shaved roughly 0.3pp off core. Meanwhile consumer spending surged 0.9% and Q2 GDP was revised up to 2.2%. The market's reaction tells the story: October hike odds collapsed from ~70% to ~37%, and the lean is now an October hold with a December hike as the live risk.

Equities held up better than rates would suggest. The S&P 500 closed Thursday at 7,666.45 (+0.19%), the Nasdaq at 26,871.60 (+0.04%), after a midday slump and a bounce as yields retreated to 5.24%. Tech outperformed the spike — semiconductors especially (Applied Materials +3.48%, Lam Research +2.86%), Micron beat and raised its outlook, and Accenture ripped 16.4%. The dollar held firm (DXY 101.73), gold sat at $4,208, and the VIX rested at 16.39 — nervous, not panicked.

The market's question, as Reuters framed it, has shifted: can "corporate earnings and economic growth keep pace with elevated borrowing costs"? Three checkpoints will answer it: today's payrolls report, October 14 CPI, and the late-October megacap earnings cluster.

## Fundamentals

### GOOGL — Alphabet · $338.24 · P/E ~17.0 (ttm) / ~16.6 (fwd) · consensus ~$429 (+27%)

The week's defining moment was Wednesday's unveiling of **Gemini 4 Argon**, Google's newest AI model. The stock popped ~1% in the session (peaking near +3% intraday), jumped ~2% in Thursday's premarket — then the rally faded entirely and Alphabet closed Thursday down 1.7%, lagging a modestly up Nasdaq. The market's verdict on model launches is getting harder to impress: the bar is no longer announcements but monetization.

The rest of the tape was regulatory. Ireland's data-protection regulator imposed a **€403M fine** over historical location-data practices; a US federal judge ruled Google must face publisher claims seeking **~$3.2B in ad-tech damages**; EU Digital Markets Act orders take effect in 2027. Offsetting some of that, Google Cloud signed a **€750M five-year Gemini collaboration with BNP Paribas**, and Piper Sandler noted TSMC is "sold out through the first half of 2028" — a direct read-through for hyperscaler capex demand (Google's own TSMC-built TPU chips began generating revenue last quarter).

**Street this week:** Piper Sandler raised its target $395 → $400 (Overweight), lifting its 2027 revenue estimate 2% and operating income estimate 8%. Still the cheapest megacap in the group on a forward multiple — ~16.6x against forward EPS growth the Street has barely marked.

### NVDA — Nvidia · $230.86 · P/E ~29.2 (ttm) / ~19.2 (fwd) · consensus ~$327.70 (+42%)

Nvidia's board authorized a **$150B share repurchase** on September 28 — roughly 2.8% of shares, taking total remaining authorization to ~$235B, called the largest in history. It is a loud statement that management views the stock as cheap, and it mechanically tightens the EPS path.

Two other threads matter. First, the FT reported Nvidia is in **early talks with insurers to underwrite financing risk on chip-collateralized loans** to "neocloud" borrowers — if chips pledged as collateral can't be resold at value. It ties to Huang's August blog on residual-value support for up to a quarter of AI opportunities, and it could unlock more than $500B in outside AI-infra capital. It also invites the circular-financing scrutiny the AI trade keeps dodging. Second, **Micron's blowout guide (~$61.5B vs ~$57B expected)** on October 1 confirmed AI infrastructure demand is still accelerating; Barclays sees hyperscaler revenue upside. Nvidia rose 1.1% Thursday to $230.86, about 2.4% below its all-time closing high. The quarterly dividend ($0.25/share) was paid October 1.

**Street this week:** Rosenblatt's Kevin Cassidy upgraded to **Buy with a $390 target** — the highest on the Street at the time — citing forward P/E of ~24x versus a five-year average of ~42x. Consensus remains Buy with ~+42% implied upside to the average target.

### AAPL — Apple (holding + watchlist) · $330.32 · P/E ~37.6 (ttm) / ~34.5 (fwd) · consensus ~$338 (+0–3%)

The paradox stock of the week. China launch-week data for the **iPhone 18 Pro was genuinely strong** — +12% year-on-year (Counterpoint), ~1.3M units in the first week, 33% weekly China smartphone share and #1 — and Apple deliberately skipped the simultaneous standard iPhone 18 launch to funnel upgrades toward pricier Pros. The stock fell ~3% the day the data hit.

Why? Two things. First, valuation: at ~34.5x forward — roughly 53% above Apple's own ten-year average P/E, and a premium to MSFT (~25x) and GOOGL (~23x) — the consensus target sits essentially *at* the current price. There is no room for "fine." Second, the Street's first real pushback on margin quality: Morgan Stanley **cut its target $360 → $355** (kept Overweight) after raising revenue estimates on stronger iPhone builds, Mac, and Services pricing — but lower iPhone ASPs and higher memory/component costs left the earnings outlook little changed. Bernstein echoed the memory-cost concern as a Q1 FY27 gross-margin risk.

The strategic debate sharpened too. BofA's Wamsi Mohan (Buy, $370) warned Apple's risk isn't losing handset sales but losing "discovery, referral, and transaction initiation" to AI agents — Meta's **Muse personal AI agent reportedly passed 5M downloads** and topped the US iOS free-app chart — while Needham's Laura Martin (Hold) argued the existential risk is an AI-first rival building an agent/hardware/monetization stack that disintermediates the iPhone, a thread running back to 2021's App Tracking Transparency (which Meta said cost it $10B a year).

Just outside the week's window but material: a September 25 jury handed Apple a **record $5.7B Taptic Engine patent verdict** for Taction Technology — the largest US patent verdict of its kind; Apple says it will appeal and infringement was not found willful (closing the door on treble damages).

**Catalysts ahead:** Apple's smart-home event (October 13 — home hub with display, HomePod mini, Apple TV, deeper Siri AI, per Gurman), the **$1,999 iPhone Duo foldable** (pre-order October 16, retail October 23; Counterpoint expects ~6M units in 2026), and Q4 FY26 earnings around **October 29** — the decisive readout on iPhone 18 demand, Services momentum, and whether memory-cost pressure shows up in margins.

### MSFT — Microsoft · $512.80 · P/E ~27.9 (ttm) / ~25–26 (fwd) · consensus ~$569–573 (+11%)

A quiet, constructive week. **Wells Fargo raised its target $700 → $725** (Overweight), added Microsoft to its **Q4 Tactical Ideas list**, and framed the stock at ~25x P/E as an earnings compounder with cloud growth to prove. Paid **Copilot seats surpassed 30 million**, attributed to the new Autopilot feature — the enterprise agent shift is now countable. Two partnership expansions (Nokia Data Suite into Microsoft Fabric; ClickHouse deeper into Fabric/OneLake/Azure) were framed as AI data-service catalysts.

The more interesting structural item: Microsoft will report under **two segments — Agents and Infra; Devices and Consumer** — starting with fiscal Q1 2027 results. The company is literally reorganizing its financial storytelling around the agentic era. On the partnership front, OpenAI completed the $10B SoftBank investment (13% stake) finalizing a $30B follow-in tranche, and Micron's guide was a positive read for Azure demand.

Context worth knowing: the stock just posted roughly a **39% September-quarter gain** — reported as the best quarter since 1991 by one outlet and since 1998 by another (sources disagree on the exact record). Either way, consolidation below $520 after that run is healthy, not alarming. **Zero sell ratings** across ~48–55 covering analysts.

### META — Meta Platforms · $725.93 · P/E ~27 (ttm) / ~22.9 (fwd) · consensus ~$788 (+8.5%)

Meta fell 3.4% on the week despite a friendlier Street. **Monness Crespi & Hardt raised its target $730 → $830** (Buy) on Tuesday, and the stock jumped 3.3% intraday on ~31%-above-average volume. Just before the window, JPMorgan went $820 → $920 (Overweight) and President Capital moved Neutral → Buy ($660 → $897). The AI-compute buildout is the justification: a reported **$27B Nebius AI-capacity agreement** this week as part of Meta's >$130B AI investment program, and the Muse personal AI agent reportedly crossing 5M downloads faster than ChatGPT.

Then came Thursday's hammer: at a post-trial hearing, **New Mexico asked Judge Francis Mathew to order Meta to pay $35–40B** after the September 25 jury verdict found the company misled consumers about Facebook data privacy (the Cambridge Analytica case; 43M+ violations alleged). The judge decides the amount; Meta argues the request doesn't match the conduct tried. Even negotiated down substantially, an order of that scale would be a major overhang event — and it caps how far multiple expansion can run while unresolved.

The stock sits ~7% below its September 24 52-week high, and roughly 17% above its 50-day moving average — an uptrend interrupted by a legal headline, not a fundamental one. Note the counterweight: OpenAI launched "Dots," a personal-AI-agent competitor, this week, so the agent race is now officially contested.

### TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor · $456.19 (ADR) · P/E ~28.4 (ttm) / ~27 (fwd) · consensus ~$552 (+20%)

The foundry that underwrites everyone else's AI ambitions had a steady week (+1.2%). TSMC **expanded its Synopsys partnership** to cover agentic/AI-assisted chip design, chiplet layouts, advanced packaging power analysis, and co-packaged optics for its **A14 process** (volume production scheduled 2028) — the design toolchain for the post-2nm era is being built now. Separately, Taiwan's UDN (via Morningstar/MarketWatch) reported TSMC is considering **Dallas, Texas for a second US chipmaking hub** alongside Phoenix; the company declined to comment. With US clients at ~75% of revenue and a $265B US commitment, the second hub is a question of when, not if. Background texture: August revenue was **NT$514.81B, +53.3% year-on-year** — the first NT$500B+ month. No new export-control or Taiwan-geopolitics developments this week. A $1.11/ADR dividend pays October 8.

### ASML — ASML Holding · $1,811.67 · P/E ~56.4 (ttm) / ~41 (fwd FY26E, computed) · consensus ~$1,970 (+8.8%)

The week's best performer in the group (+3.9%) on a **Wall Street reiteration cluster**: UBS reiterated Buy with a **€2,350 target**, BofA reiterated Buy, Barclays maintained Buy — three buy calls in a short stretch ahead of Q3 earnings, with UBS noting ASML's 2027–28 lithography capacity targets may move above 30% growth. The stock rose ~3% intraday Monday to $1,815.84. The company also reported its weekly buyback activity: **165,200 shares for ~€248M** (September 21–25) under the €12B program running to end-2028.

The technology story keeps compounding: construction began on the Eindhoven Brainport Industries Campus North (350k sqm, phase one 2029), and **Samsung joined the 12-inch High-NA EUV photomask initiative**, planning High-NA EUV in DRAM high-volume manufacturing by 2028. High-NA is moving from ASML's roadmap to its customers' roadmaps. Up ~69% year-to-date, ASML remains the highest-multiple name in the group at ~56x trailing — you are paying for a monopoly on the machines that make the machines.

### AMZN — Amazon · $248.23 · P/E ~19.6 (ttm) / ~26.5 (fwd) · consensus ~$322–330 (+30–33%)

Amazon was the week's analyst darling and the tape's laggard — down 0.6% while the Street piled in. **Goldman Sachs added AMZN to its US Conviction List** on Thursday: Eric Sheridan reiterated Buy with a **$375 target** (~+50% upside), citing multi-year revenue compounding, margin expansion, AI-compute demand, and the embryonic ad platform, with Goldman's earnings forecast 8% above the Street. **Rosenblatt raised $335 → $360** (Buy), calling the "AI agents erode retail media" narrative false — Amazon's marketplace/fulfillment/retail-media moat is defensible (notably after Amazon blocked Meta's Muse commerce agent). **BofA raised $286 → $320**. Fifty-six of 59 analysts are Buy or equivalent, and the consensus implies ~+30–33% upside — the widest gap between price and target in the group.

The infrastructure buildout was tangible: **AWS signed a >$1B multiyear deal with Synopsys** for chip-design IP (Trainium/Graviton), and Amazon signed a **20-year agreement with Constellation Energy for 690 MW of nuclear power** at Calvert Cliffs (>$3B investment) for data-center expansion. Anthropic reportedly plans to commit >$100B to AWS over time. The week's softer items: **Amazon Ads' unBoxed 2026** (AI-centered: Ads Agent platform, Full-Funnel Campaigns), Bezos at the White House AI self-regulation meeting, and the **FTC Prime settlement payouts beginning October 1** (up to $200 automatic payments from the $2.5B fund).

## Macro watch

**The Fed.** Next meeting **October 27–28** (decision Wednesday the 28th), then December 8–9. The September 15–16 meeting delivered a hike to **3.75–4.00%** — the first in three years — with a median projection (4.1% end-2026) implying one more hike this year. This week's repricing was dramatic: October hike odds fell from ~70% to **~37%** after the cooler-than-feared August PCE. The speaker tape is split: Williams (Sep 29) sees "no need for urgency"; Jefferson (Oct 1) wants "more time"; Barr says further adjustments are "likely," explicitly citing AI spending pushing up chip prices; Kashkari (Oct 1) "doesn't know how high rates will have to go." September FOMC minutes drop **Wednesday, October 7**. Pre-meeting blackout begins around October 17.

**Inflation.** September CPI lands **Wednesday, October 14** (8:30 AM ET) — the make-or-break input for the October 28 decision. No credible consensus was published at research time; reference points: August printed 3.4% headline (+0.4% m/m) and 2.4% core (+0.3% m/m). A hot core print re-prices toward a hike; a cool one locks in the hold. The Beige Book drops the same afternoon.

**Labor.** The September employment report is **today — Friday, October 2, 8:30 AM ET** (after this letter's research window). Consensus is ~90k payrolls (FactSet ~95k, Dow Jones ~84k), unemployment 4.1%, wages +0.3% m/m — a hiring plateau after August's big +162k beat. This is the only major labor read before the October FOMC. Weekly claims are healthy (197k; continuing claims 1.7M, lowest since March 2023).

**The calendar around it:** ISM Services (Oct 5), trade balance (Oct 6), FOMC minutes (Oct 7), jobless claims (Oct 8), Michigan sentiment (Oct 9), PPI + retail sales (Oct 15), industrial production (Oct 16). Just beyond the three-week window: **Q3 GDP advance on October 28** — the same morning as the FOMC decision — and September PCE on October 29.

**What it means for these names.** The AI trade is simultaneously the market's shield and the rate problem's source: megacap tech outperformed the yield spike, yet AI capex is, in the Fed's own telling, adding to the demand for capital and raising borrowing costs. Duration-sensitive laggards (AMZN, ~14% off highs, $220B capex program) feel a hot October 14 CPI most; cash-rich compounders (MSFT, GOOGL, META) are more insulated but not immune. For the semis, Barr's chip-price remarks are a reminder that the AI capex boom is now a monetary-policy variable — the October 14–15 TSM/ASML prints land squarely in that debate.
