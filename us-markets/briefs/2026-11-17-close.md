## Why it fell

- **Commerce shut the "cloud loophole" (the day's trigger).** The Commerce Department issued an interim final rule (RIN 0694-AK50) requiring licences for remote access to advanced AI chips by firms in China and other D:5 countries — data centres anywhere in the world need a licence if they supply more than 12 million aggregate TPP (roughly 750 H100s / ~330 B200s) of compute to Chinese-headquartered firms — plus licence requirements for advanced-chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, named as diversion risks. The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. Existing operators have 90 days to get licences; it takes effect on Federal Register publication. Bloomberg tied the move directly to the scrutiny after the Nvidia/Megaspeed espionage scandal (TechCrunch, Fortune via Stockvolt, ZeroHedge). This is the single biggest NVDA/TSM/ASML overhang of the day: it puts a licensing cloud over a chunk of the AI-chip demand chain, four days after Huang's resignation.
- **Asia chip rout overnight.** Taiwan's Taiex fell 6.03% — its second-largest point drop on record — with TSMC Taipei -5.74% to NT$2,215 (over 40% of index weight, ~1,000 points of the drop); Kospi -3.59%, Nikkei -2.42%. Foreign institutions sold a net NT$70.19B of Taiwan shares (CNA/Focus Taiwan). TSM's ADR simply followed Taipei down and then some.
- **NVDA earnings tomorrow — de-risking plus target cuts.** NVDA reports Tue Nov 17 AMC (cons EPS ~$2.52, rev ~$111.2B; options imply ~±8.6%), its first print under interim CEO Colette Kress. Analysts cut targets into the print: UBS Buy $300→$240, Mizuho Outperform $250→$220, TD Cowen Buy $275→$200, CLSA set $255 (MarketBeat). Treasury Secretary Bessent's weekend demand — hire an outside CEO, explain within 10 days whether the board pushed export-suspect transactions to lift revenue — is still hanging over the name.
- **Macro: Empire State missed, Warsh said nothing on rates.** Nov Empire State manufacturing printed 7.8 vs ~16.7–18 expected (Oct 25.5, strongest since Dec 2021) — a sharp deceleration (Zacks). Fed Chair Warsh's noon remarks at the San Francisco Fed productivity conference were all AI, no monetary policy — no hint on the December hike the market has fully priced (InvestingLive). No relief, no new damage.
- **The tape:** tech was the weakest S&P sector (-1.6%); staples and health care each +0.8% (BigGo/Reuters). Beyond the universe: Intel -5.2 to -6.4%, Micron -5.3 to -5.8%, Broadcom -4.4%, AMD -2.2 to -4.7%, CoreWeave -6.5%. Nine of 11 S&P sectors finished lower.
