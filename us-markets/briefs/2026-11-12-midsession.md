## ⚠️ NVDA −4.80% — Jensen Huang resigns as CEO; seven downgrades in 24 hours

**What happened:** Nvidia announced this morning that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, ending his 33-year run. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO (keeping the CFO role) and board member Harvey C. Jones becomes Nvidia's first-ever board chair; the board is searching for a permanent successor, internal and external (Nvidia press release via USA Today / CNN / Nikkei). The resignation follows the affair revelations — a relationship with a woman US officials believe is a Chinese spy, plus a New York Times report of a second affair with a company executive — and the board's probe into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. The board had initially expressed confidence in Huang on Sunday, then met Wednesday night after the NYT report to weigh removing him; the Wall Street Journal reported Nov 11 that directors were preparing to oust him, and the stock fell ~8% in after-hours trading.

The market reaction has been brutal: the stock opened −13% at $151.01, hit an intraday low of $148.80 (its lowest since late June 2025, below the prior 52-week low of $164.27), then clawed back to ~$165 by midday — still −4.8% and ~−32% from the Oct 6 high of $243.37. Market cap is ~$4.07T (Finnhub), down ~$1.58T from the $5.76T peak on Oct 6. Seven brokerages downgraded the stock between Wednesday and Thursday morning (Baird, Oppenheimer, Bernstein, Evercore ISI, William Blair, KeyBanc, Melius) and price targets were roughly halved: Bernstein $400→$200, Baird $500→$200, Evercore $465→$186, Melius $315→$185; Cantor kept Overweight but cut $350→$250 and removed NVDA from its top picks ("A Very Sad Day"); Citi cut to $225, Stifel to $205, RBC to $230, Mizuho to $250. Dissenters: Raymond James kept Strong Buy, $515 ("Nvidia is more than just its CEO"), Morgan Stanley kept Overweight $300 (but dropped it from recommended lists), BofA kept Buy $350, Morningstar held its $310 fair value. Dan Ives put it at $225 without Huang vs $300 with him. Q3 earnings are still scheduled as planned for Tuesday Nov 17 (~1:20 PM PT); the House briefing is Nov 18.

**Koda's read:** The repricing is *understandable, not yet a verdict*. Huang was the architect of the AI story and the stock carried a founder premium — a forced exit amid a national-security probe is a genuine governance overhang, and the Street's target cuts (many to ~$200) reflect that. But nothing about the business changed overnight: the board says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap continue without interruption, and earnings land in five days. The intraday tape tells the story — a −13% panic open to $148.80, then a sharp recovery to −4.8% — which looks like capitulation, not orderly exit. **Hold — don't sell into the panic, and don't buy yet either.** The stock is sitting right on its old 52-week low (~$164.27); a close below it opens more downside, while a reclaim of ~$180–186 would signal stabilisation. The real test is the Nov 17 earnings call: what Kress says about demand, and any word on the permanent-CEO search. Until then, this is a headline stock, not a fundamentals stock.

## ⚠️ GOOGL +2.06% — rotation bid as money leaves Nvidia

**What happened:** No fresh Google-specific catalyst today. The +2% is a rotation move: with NVDA in freefall, money is rotating into traditional mega-cap tech — Apple and Alphabet both gained overnight as NVDA slid (Stocktwits), and GOOGL held its gains through the morning, trading up to a $300.40 high. The carryover driver is Monday's TPU launch (TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max), which the Motley Fool flagged as putting Google in closer competition with Nvidia, and a Yahoo piece today calling Alphabet's TPUs a "hidden catalyst." Against the grain, Arete Research cut its GOOGL target to $370 from $450 on Tuesday.

**Koda's read:** The move is *rotation, not re-rating* — understandable given the NVDA shock, and GOOGL is the cheapest mega-cap on the board (P/E ~15x trailing), so it is the natural landing spot. But +2% on no new company news, with a fresh target cut this week, is not a buy signal. **Hold — don't chase.** GOOGL is still ~−15% over the past month and ~27% below its $408.61 52-week high; the TPU story is real but slow-burning, and the $300 level is the first test.
