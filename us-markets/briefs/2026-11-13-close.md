The rest of the tape split the same way it has all week: money rotated out of the AI epicentre into traditional mega-cap tech (GOOGL +1.8%, MSFT +0.4%) and into Nvidia's rivals (AMD and Broadcom each +~5%), while the chip supply chain took collateral damage (ASML −2.4%, TSM −1.6%). ## Why it moved

### NVDA — $166.19, -4.34% (holding)
The day the founder premium came off. Nvidia announced before the open that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, ending his 33-year run. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO (keeping the CFO role) and board member Harvey C. Jones becomes Nvidia's first-ever board chair; the board is searching for a permanent successor, internal and external. The resignation follows the affair revelations — a relationship with a woman US officials believe is a Chinese spy, plus a New York Times report of a second affair with a company executive — and the board's probe into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. The board had expressed confidence in Huang on Sunday, then met Wednesday night after the NYT report; the Wall Street Journal reported Nov 11 that directors were preparing to oust him.

The tape was violent: the stock opened −13% at $151.01, hit an intraday low of $148.80 — a new 52-week low, below the prior $164.27 — then clawed back all session to close at $166.19, $17.39 off the low and just above that old 52-week low. Volume was ~642M shares, ~4.4x the 30-day average. Market cap is ~$4.07T, down ~$1.7T from the $5.76T peak on Oct 6; the stock is −17.0% over five sessions, −27.3% over a month and −10.9% year-to-date. After hours it ticked up to $167.03 (+0.50%).

The Street's verdict landed fast: seven brokerages downgraded between Wednesday and Thursday morning (Baird, Oppenheimer, Bernstein, Evercore ISI, William Blair, KeyBanc, Melius) and targets were roughly halved — Bernstein $400→$200 ("we've had enough"), Baird $500→$200, Evercore $465→$186, Melius $315→$185, Needham $300→$200, Cantor $350→$250. Goldman cut to $220 from $300 but kept Buy, calling the departure a "meaningful, but measured negative" with a measured impact on financials over 12–18 months given the long-dated backlog; Citi cut to $225, Stifel to $205, RBC to $230, Mizuho to $250, StoneX to Hold with its target suspended. The dissenters held their ground: Raymond James kept Strong Buy at $515 ("Nvidia is more than just its CEO"), Morgan Stanley kept Overweight $300, BofA kept Buy $350, Morningstar held its $310 fair value, Wedbush kept Outperform at $345, New Street reiterated Buy. Dan Ives put it at $225 without Huang vs $300 with him. And in the afternoon, President Trump suggested on Truth Social that Apple's former CEO Tim Cook should replace Huang (Investor's Business Daily).

**Koda's read:** A capitulation-shaped day, not an orderly exit — a −13% panic open to a new 52-week low, then a $17 recovery on the heaviest volume of the year. The stock closed above the old 52-week low ($164.27), which matters. But the governance overhang is real and the Street's new target cluster ($185–250) is a long way from the old $400–500 world. Nothing about the business changed overnight — operations, customer commitments and the roadmap continue, per the board — and earnings land Tuesday Nov 17 (cons EPS ~$2.53, rev ~$111.4B), the first results under interim CEO Kress. **Lean: HOLD — don't sell into the panic, and don't buy yet either.** Watch $164.27 below and $180–186 above; the Nov 17 call is the real test.

### GOOGL — $298.70, +1.79% (holding)
The rotation's landing spot, for the second day running. No fresh Google-specific catalyst — the +1.8% is money leaving Nvidia and landing in the cheapest mega-cap on the board (P/E ~15x trailing), with Monday's TPU launch (TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max) still in the background as the "hidden catalyst" case. It traded to a $302.56 high, closing just under $300; after hours it ticked to $299.23. Against the grain, Arete Research cut its target to $370 from $450 on Tuesday.

**Koda's read:** Rotation, not re-rating — two green days totalling ~+3.9% on no new company news, with a fresh target cut this week. **Lean: HOLD — don't chase.** $300 is the level to watch; a close above it with volume would be the first real test. Still ~−15.6% over the past month and ~27% below the $408.61 52-week high.

### AAPL — $354.84, -0.22% (holding)

### MSFT — $523.00, +0.36% (holding)
