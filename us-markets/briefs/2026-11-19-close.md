## Why it moved

- **FOMC minutes: hawkish, and the tape faded after them.** The minutes of the Oct 27–28 meeting (released 2:00 PM ET) show "almost all" officials judged further rate increases would likely be appropriate; some favoured a hike in October, a few who backed the hold called it a close call, and "a couple" noted an increase of more than 25bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data proved unfavourable. The AI buildout featured prominently — officials called it a material source of inflation risk and flagged financial-stability concerns. The S&P gave back its afternoon rebound after the release. Context: the Oct 28 hold was 10–2 (Cook and Hammack dissenting for +25bp); October CPI then printed hot (+0.4% headline, +0.3% core). A December 25bp hike is ~95% priced (CME FedWatch); 50bp odds sit at ~5–10%, with >80bp of tightening priced through September 2027.
- **Waller's 50bp call set the tone before the minutes.** Fed Governor Christopher Waller said Wednesday morning (CNBC) that the Fed may need to "shock people" with a half-point hike at the Dec 8–9 meeting, or clearly signal a faster pace of hikes in 2027 — inflation is "headed in the wrong direction" and broadening, with ~70% of core services items rising above 3%. NY Fed President Williams countered that another increase would probably be needed but pushed back on an aggressive path. The 2-year yield rose to ~4.83% on the remarks.
- **Nvidia: the beat-and-raise that keeps selling off.** NVDA opened +2.4% at $159.19, touched $159.70, then reversed all of it and more to close -2.89% at $150.96, with an intraday low of $150.40 — $1.60 above the Nov 12 52-week low of $148.80. The stock is now ~38% below its Oct 6 high ($243.37) and -10.6% over five sessions. The numbers are not the problem (Q3 rev $115.84B, +103% YoY; Q4 guide mid ~$130B; FY28 growth raised to ~75%); the market is pricing a leadership discount. Wall Street split today: Piper Sandler cut its target to $230 from $300 (Overweight kept; "business as usual" call, expects the stock range-bound until CEO clarity), Jefferies $300→$250 (Buy), Wells Fargo $300→$250 (Buy), UBS $240→$220 (Buy), Barclays $275→$210, DA Davidson $300→$200, Evercore $186→$184 (Hold), Summit Insights downgraded to Hold — while JPMorgan raised to $230 from $215 (Overweight), Wedbush raised to $293 from $275 (Outperform), Morgan Stanley upgraded to Buy (PT $267→$260), and BMO/Argus/RBC reiterated bullish. The House closed-door briefing on the Huang probe was today; the board is not considering reinstatement.
- **Amazon: the day's clear winner.** AMZN +3.82% to $267.74, holding near its $268.29 high — the best day in the universe and its second strong session in a row. The stack from mid-session held: (1) Blackstone agreed to buy a 75% stake in two AWS data-centre joint ventures for ~$5B (13 halls, 647 MW), AWS keeping 25% and leasing back — a capital-light answer to the ~$220B 2026 capex plan; (2) Nvidia's print showed the largest cloud companies paid it $60.1B in Q3 (+71% YoY), validating hyperscaler AI spend; (3) UBS initiated Buy, $340 PT, on Nov 17; (4) Anthropic prices tonight and Amazon holds ~14.7% of it.
- **The Dow's problem was healthcare, not tech.** UnitedHealth plunged ~9% (the Dow's biggest decliner) after the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services proposed a rule widening audits of Medicare and Medicaid payments; Humana fell ~10%, pacing S&P 500 decliners. UNH carries a ~4.3% weight in the price-weighted Dow, so one stock did much of the damage. Retail was the offset: TJX jumped ~11% and Target ~7% on beats and raised guidance (Lowe's flat after its own beat).
- **Anthropic prices tonight.** The IPO is still officially marketed at $70–78 for 825M shares (~$64.4B at the top, plus a $26B private placement to seven strategic investors including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC — up to ~$90.4B combined). The WSJ reported Nov 17 that advisers were considering a price above $80; CNBC reported Nov 18 that Anthropic was weighing a price well above $80 and a larger deal. Trading begins Thursday on Nasdaq as ANT. No pricing filing had appeared by the close; the final price lands in the morning brief.
