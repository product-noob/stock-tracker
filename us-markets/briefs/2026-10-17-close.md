## Why the tape looked the way it did

- **AI-debt fear, not inflation, drove Friday** (CNN: "Nasdaq sinks more than 2% as investors raise questions about AI debt"): **CoreWeave dropped 14%** — Huang's Thursday Bloomberg endorsement proved short-lived; credit-default-swap costs hit records. D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria: CoreWeave is "the subprime tranche of the AI trade." CRWV down one-quarter this week, −60%+ since its March 2025 IPO peak
- **The market is splitting AI names into two trades** (tradingnews): firms that self-fund capex from operating cash (Nvidia, Apple) vs firms that depend on bond markets (CoreWeave, neoclouds) — "in a market with a 5.09% 10-year yield the second group is being treated as rate-sensitive"
- **China-memory surprise added to chip pain** (CNN): CXMT's advanced-memory headway earlier this week roiled memory pricing-power bets — Micron −3.8%, SK hynix ADRs −5.3%, Kospi −14.5% on the week (worst since 2008, wiping six months of gains)

- **Next week is the real test**: META reports Wednesday Oct 21, then GOOGL/MSFT on Oct 28 and AMZN on Oct 29 — tradingnews: "Warsh's neutral tone and a likely October pause give investors time to see next week's Big Tech earnings"

## Koda's read

Friday was the AI-credit trade at full roar: the market now treats anything levered to data-center debt as a credit trade, not an equity trade — CoreWeave −14% and a VIX +30% are the tells. The relative-strength board still points the same way: AAPL (lone green, shielded by cash and zero neocloud exposure) and NVDA (funds its own capex) are being separated from the financed-buildout names. META's −3.91% into the $700s is pre-earnings de-risking on top of the selloff — it's now a coin flip: a clean Oct 21 print could snap it back, a capex miss could take it to $650. TSM holding above $400 with three PT raises underneath is the watchlist buy-zone forming — if it dips toward $400 next week and holds, that's the entry.
