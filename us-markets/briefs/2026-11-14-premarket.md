## Weekend developments

**NVDA — the succession story moved again.** The Financial Times reported Friday that Nvidia's board has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a possible successor to Jensen Huang. The board says speculation is premature — it is still selecting a search firm and has not formally engaged any candidate — and Haas is one of several names under consideration, with no guarantee he is offered the job or accepts it. Meanwhile the push to bring Huang back is building: two prominent investors, Altimeter's Brad Gerstner and Atreides' Gavin Baker, wrote to the board urging it not to rush a permanent appointment and to keep Huang's return on the table; Elon Musk said on X he would sign a petition to reinstate Huang; and employees have been pressing the same case internally. Internal candidates mentioned include interim CEO Colette Kress and CUDA architect Ian Buck. The stock enters the weekend at $162.85, −10.3% on the week (its worst week in over a year), −33% below the Oct 6 high of $243.37, market cap ~$3.9T — and it reports Tuesday.

**MSFT — the outage is over.** Microsoft said Saturday that its global Microsoft 365 outage is fully resolved, ending a disruption that ran more than 24 hours from Thursday afternoon (Outlook, Teams, Copilot; 7.4M+ Downdetector reports across 70+ countries). Cause: a network capacity upgrade issue that surfaced after a subsequent network change — not cyber, not AI. The stock closed Friday +0.17% at $523.88 despite the outage entering its second day. Ex-dividend Thursday Nov 19 ($0.98).

**META — bond deal done.** Meta completed a €9B six-tranche bond offering on Friday, per the Friday market recap — the debt-funded buildout story, now funded. The stock was Friday's best in the universe (+2.90% to $641.69) on no company news; it has gained ~6% over five sessions.

**AAPL — $5.2T and still the shelter.** Apple closed at $356.27 after a second straight intraday record ($360.83), lifting market value above $5.2T as money rotates toward names seen as outside the AI-infrastructure race.

**The AI trade, 30 days on.** One tally of 47 AI-linked names has the group down ~$3.9T in market value since Oct 14, with Nvidia alone accounting for ~$1.4T of it; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is down ~13% over the same stretch, the Nasdaq-100 ~7%, the S&P 500 ~2%. Money didn't leave tech — it fled anything whose value depends on the data-center buildout continuing at its current pace.
