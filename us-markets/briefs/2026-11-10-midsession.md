## ALERT — NVDA −6.3% ($167.37): the probe sell-off accelerates

**What happened.** Day two of the Jensen Huang investigation cascade, and the selling got worse, not better. NVDA opened near $174 after a ~2% pre-market bounce, then slid all session to its lowest level since late March (Morningstar) — now ~31% below its October 6 high of $243.37. It was the worst performer in the Dow and the most actively traded S&P 500 stock (WSJ). Fresh items since yesterday's close:

- **Shareholder class action** filed Monday after the close in federal court in San Jose, accusing Nvidia and Huang of misleading investors by concealing the relationship and its national-security implications (Reuters).
- **House Foreign Affairs Committee Democrats** wrote to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick calling for a halt to advanced AI-chip exports to China, demanding answers by December 1 on whether Commerce knew of the intelligence linking Alice Huang — a former Megaspeed executive — to Chinese efforts to obtain Nvidia chips (Reuters via Jerusalem Post).
- **Analyst target cuts today:** Citi cut its price target to $285 from $315 (Buy reiterated); Wells Fargo cut to $300 from $315 (Overweight kept); Bank of America removed NVDA — along with Micron and Lam Research — from its highest-conviction US 1 List, while keeping its Buy rating and $350 target (Investor's Business Daily; KuCoin). Per IBD, three analysts have cut targets since the weekend and one downgraded to neutral. Goldman reiterated Buy ($300) and RBC Outperform ($330) on Monday.
- **The political backdrop widened:** Trump confirmed Monday the FBI is looking into Huang's relationship, while saying he believes Huang ("I had no idea she was a spy") and that Huang will be "in good shape." The NYT reported Trump was briefed on the intelligence about a year ago and chose not to act. China denied the spying claims. Nvidia's board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised; Huang has apologised to employees and says he will cooperate fully. Reuters' analysis flags the governance risk: Huang, 63, has no obvious successor and ~60 direct reports.

**Koda's read.** Still a governance/leadership-risk discount, not a business event — no evidence has emerged that secrets were compromised or that chip demand has changed. But the discount is compounding for a reason: the FBI probe, a class action, and a House push to halt China chip exports each extend the timeline and widen the tail risk, and Wall Street is now marking targets down rather than defending them. A ~31% fall from the October high also carries the pre-existing AI-valuation unwind (the stock was already down ~27% over the past month before the weekend). The move looks overdone on fundamentals alone, but uninvestable on headlines alone — the two facts coexist. **Lean: HOLD — do not add into the headlines.** Earnings on Tuesday Nov 17 (cons EPS ~$2.53, revenue ~$111.4B) is the next real catalyst, and the House briefing is Nov 18. Levels: $164.27 (52-week low, March 30) is the line — a break opens $160; reclaiming Monday's $178.67 close would signal stabilisation.
