## Why it moved

### The macro: CoreWeave, not CPI
No economic data and no bond market — Veterans Day left the tape to earnings. CoreWeave reported after Tuesday's bell and the numbers were a demand datapoint the AI trade badly needed: Q3 revenue $3.66B, up 168% y/y and above the $3.54B FactSet consensus; adjusted operating income $379M, far above its own $200–260M guidance; revenue backlog a record $132.5B (from $104.2B at end-Q2); full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to $13.0–13.3B, above the $12.82B consensus; year-end annualised revenue run-rate target raised to $20–21B. CEO Michael Intrator said 2028 capacity is already sold out and the company expects ~$9B of customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months. The stock jumped ~26% and the whole AI infrastructure complex followed — Micron +12%, AMD and Broadcom +5%+, Intel +9.5%, memory names +10–16%. The caveats stayed in the filing, not the tape: a $709M net loss, $846M of quarterly interest expense on $46.9B of debt, and Q4 interest guided to $1.05–1.15B. For today, demand beat debt.

### NVDA — $173.73, +2.87% (holding)
First gain in three sessions, snapping the two-day -9.5% slide — but the bounce faded from a +5% premarket and a $177.54 intraday high, closing nearer the day's middle. The CoreWeave relief rally did the lifting; the analyst tape was genuinely split. On the positive side, Raymond James upgraded to Strong Buy from Outperform, and Morgan Stanley reiterated Overweight ($300) — though it also removed NVDA from its Top Pick list. On the negative side, Baird downgraded to Neutral and more than halved its target, $500 → $200; Jefferies initiated coverage at Hold; Evercore cut Outperform → Positive. In Washington, the pressure kept building: Sen. Kevin Cramer (Senate Banking) told Politico late Tuesday that Huang should resign, calling the extortion risk "tremendous", and Larry Kudlow urged the same on Fox Business. The House deadline expired at end of day Wednesday with no public response from Huang as of the close — House China chair Moolenaar said on Fox Business that Huang must answer by end of day, and Foreign Affairs chair Mast said his committee is preparing a subpoena if he does not (TechCrunch/Bloomberg). The Nov 18 closed-door briefing, the day after earnings, is still the date to watch. The board's probe, led by former US attorney Joon Kim of Wachtell Lipton, continues; the Rosen class action (Kistenmacher v. Nvidia, N.D. Cal., filed Nov 9) is now in the mix.

**Koda's read:** A relief bounce, not a clearing — +2.9% on 2x volume after -9.5% in two days, with the stock still ~29% below its Oct 6 record ($243.37) and -16% over five sessions. The Raymond James upgrade matters, but so does the Baird cut to $200, and the Washington clock is still ticking. **Lean: HOLD — the bounce is buyable only if you believe the probe stays a governance story;** earnings Nov 17 is the next real catalyst and the only one that can change the subject. Watch $170.24 (Monday's low) below and $178.67 (Monday's close) above.

### TSM — $413.35, +4.25% (watchlist)
The day's best name in the universe, and the second green day running. Two drivers stacked: the CoreWeave-led chip rally, and TSMC's own board meeting. The board approved up to $500M to buy Anthropic common shares at the IPO price and up to $500M of OpenAI preferred stock — a direct financial stake in the two companies driving demand for its leading-edge capacity — plus $40.93B of capital appropriations for advanced manufacturing, packaging, plant construction and 2027 R&D, and a Q3 dividend raised to NT$9 from NT$7. Q3 revenue was $45.50B with net income $23.23B. Anthropic's IPO is priced $70–78 a share, aiming to raise $61B+, with a $26B concurrent private placement from seven giants including Google, Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC; pricing is set for Nov 18.

**Koda's read:** The strongest name in the tape two days running — October revenue +59.7% y/y on Tuesday, a $1B strategic bet on its own customers on Wednesday. **Lean: WATCHLIST — constructive;** $407 is now support, $417 (today's high) the next test. The Anthropic/OpenAI stakes are small in dollars but a loud signal about where TSMC thinks demand is going.

### GOOGL — $293.45, +2.11% (holding)
Back above $290 and holding most of the gain into the close. The driver is the Anthropic S-1, amended Nov 10: Google will invest another $10B in Anthropic at the IPO price, on top of its existing stake — at the $74 midpoint that lifts Google's Class N holding to ~1.79B shares, ~13.7% of Anthropic's outstanding Class A and N shares, worth ~$132.4B, or ~3.8% of Alphabet's market value (Motley Fool analysis of the filing). Anthropic booked $33.2B of revenue over nine months against $900.9B of computing commitments, and market chatter has the IPO valuation rising toward $1.5T on strong demand. Google also has new TPU variants and Gemini 4 Carbon in the background, plus large Google Cloud commitments from Anthropic itself.

**Koda's read:** The Anthropic stake is becoming a real line item — ~$132B of value at the IPO midpoint, and it cuts both ways: it validates Google's AI position, but Anthropic's new Claude Sheets also competes with Google Sheets. **Lean: HOLD.** $290 is support again; $296.33 (Monday's high) is the next test.

### ASML — $1,534.42, +1.76% (watchlist)
A solid but unremarkable day — it rode the semiconductor rally (SOXX ~+3%) without company-specific news. The background remains the 2027–28 supply story: ASML is sounding out whether it can push 2028 capacity beyond the 30% expansion already flagged, after warning that planned output of Low-NA EUV and DUV immersion systems will fall short of customer demand. The Nikkei's weekend Terafab story (Musk targeting 1M wafers/month by 2032) is still in the mix. Q3 was €11.7B of sales and ~€5B of net income; Q4 is guided to €14.2–15.2B.

**Koda's read:** A beta move, not a thesis move — but it held above $1,500 on a day the whole complex bounced. **Lean: WATCH — do not chase;** $1,450–1,480 remains the better entry zone on any pullback.

### META — $628.77, -1.97% (watchlist)
The day's ugly reversal in the universe: it opened at $646.74, near the highs, and faded all session to close at $628.77, a whisker above its $627.97 low. The news was a leadership change: longtime infrastructure chief Santosh Janardhan — also co-head of engineering — will step down in early 2027, with Dave Brown, a former AWS senior vice president who joined Meta earlier this year, succeeding him. Janardhan stays through Q1 2027 to manage the transition and will serve as CTO of Meta Compute (Reuters/Bloomberg). No reason was given in the internal memo. The stock also gave back part of Monday's +5.12% JPMorgan gain, and Meta is not among the seven Anthropic IPO anchor investors.

**Koda's read:** A leadership change at the top of the AI infrastructure build is not nothing, even with an orderly handover — and the intraday shape (open high, close at the low) says sellers were in control. **Lean: WATCHLIST — no chase;** $600 is support; a close back above $641 (Tuesday's close) would repair the damage.

### MSFT — $521.13, -2.26% (holding)
The worst name in the universe for the second straight day, and the divergence is now the story: -2.26% on a day the Nasdaq-100 rose 2.31%. Two specific pressures, both from Tuesday, carried over. First, Anthropic's Claude Sheets, Whiteboards and Data launch — a spreadsheet tool that looks a lot like Excel, aimed squarely at the $27.8B Microsoft 365 business — has revived the February "SaaSpocalypse" software scare; the stock fell 2.2% on Tuesday on the same news and another 2.3% today. Second, Reuters reported Nov 10 that Microsoft is lobbying the Trump administration to drop a proposed ban on Chinese optical transceivers for data centres — a supply-cost and buildout risk for a company planning 35 US data centres now and 134 more. The AI capex bill ($45.2B last quarter, $50B+ expected this quarter) is the third, quieter weight. The irony: Microsoft is one of Anthropic's seven IPO anchor investors, and Anthropic has agreed to pay Microsoft up to $49.2B for compute through 2033 — the disruptor is also a customer. RBC reiterated Outperform with a $640 target on Tuesday. Ex-dividend Nov 19 ($0.98).

**Koda's read:** This is now a stock-specific de-rating, not tape — two down days totalling -4.5% while the market rallied. The Anthropic threat to Office is real enough to price, but Microsoft's free cash flow ($18.6B last quarter, still positive after leases) is what separates it from the February panic, when buying the dip returned ~30% by Tuesday. **Lean: HOLD — do not add while the software scare is repricing, but the cash-flow case against selling into it is intact.** $517.75 is support; $533 (Tuesday's close) is now resistance.

### AMZN — $266.17, -1.74% (watchlist)
The third red name on a green tape day, and like MSFT and META it faded from a higher open ($273.52) to close near its low ($265.62). No fresh company catalyst: the available coverage identifies no new event (WhyIs). The overhang is the same one from the Oct 29 print — AWS grew 39% y/y, its fastest in five years, but $220B of 2026 capex pushed trailing free cash flow to -$42.4B, and the market is still deciding whether to pay for AI spending on faith. Small positives (delivery-driver smart glasses rollout, Amazon Haul holiday deals) didn't move it. Note the cross-current: Amazon is Anthropic's largest Class N holder (~1.92B shares), ahead of Google — the IPO pop, if it comes, accrues to Amazon too.

**Koda's read:** Rotation, not a re-think — money went into silicon, not into the cloud-and-retail names, and Amazon's capex bill makes it the easiest to leave behind on a chip day. **Lean: WATCHLIST — constructive on AWS, patient on price;** $260 is support; $275 (Monday's high) is the next test.
