# Market-close recap — Friday Oct 23, 2026 (4:00 PM ET)

**Two tapes in one.** Tech caught its breath after Thursday's AI-spend rout — the **Nasdaq Composite rose +0.21%** to 26,062.75 and a gauge of chipmakers rebounded **~+1%** (Reuters) — while a fresh oil shock torched the rest of the market: **Brent climbed 2.74% to $116.27, a five-month high**, on more Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia (an Aramco facility at Yanbu, a tanker spill off the UAE, and the Dhahran headquarters evacuated). **WTI rose 2.56% to ~$102.02.** The result: the **S&P 500 eased −0.14%** to 7,652.96 (348 of its 503 constituents red, per CosAnalyst), and the **Dow fell −0.9%** to 52,391.73. Treasury yields rose after their best day in a year (~5.01%), as the inflation print from crude ran headlong into the Oct 28 FOMC decision. Bank of America's Michael Hartnett put out a tactically risk-off note Friday: down-in-yields, down-in-stocks, down-in-US-dollar "until mid-Nov," citing AI-trade jitters and midterm risk.

Elsewhere in the tape: **American Express fell ~3.5%** after results (raised profit forecast, but the CEO's "more agile and efficient" efficiency push read as cautious), and telecoms got hit by a Bloomberg report that **SpaceX is in talks with cellular-infrastructure firms to launch a nationwide wireless carrier** — T/VZ/TMUS −3 to −4.7% premarket while **Crown Castle jumped ~12%** and American Tower +6%. The Motley Fool's midday read: energy and utilities were the only sectors leading; cybersecurity caught a bid on AI-threat fears.
