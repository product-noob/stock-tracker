## Weekend news check — what changed since the mid-session brief

- **NVDA / Huang — he has agreed to testify; no date set.** The weekend's headline development: House Select Committee on China chairman John Moolenaar told CBS's *Face the Nation* on Sunday that Jensen Huang has agreed to answer lawmakers' questions privately about his relationship with Alice Huang, an alleged Chinese spy, before members of three House committees (China, Foreign Affairs, Intelligence). No date has been set, and Moolenaar said Huang's voluntary cooperation removes the need for a subpoena (Seeking Alpha, Bloomberg Law, TradingView). This resolves the standoff the pre-market brief left open — the House's 8 PM ET Friday deadline for a written Dec 1 commitment passed with no confirmed acceptance — but it is an agreement in principle, not a scheduled Dec 1 appearance. Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott still wants Huang in public on Dec 3 and Commerce Secretary Lutnick on Dec 10 (Lutnick has agreed to appear).
- **NVDA / Huang — Reuters: he misled Nvidia's top lawyer.** Reuters reported Sunday, citing internal messages from October and two people familiar with the matter, that Huang told Nvidia's general counsel and other senior leaders his dealings with Alice Huang were ordinary customer business while the two were having an affair (Reuters, via South Bay Chronicle). It keeps the probe narrative live into Tuesday's earnings call, where interim CEO Colette Kress will be pressed on the board/FBI/House tracks.
- **NVDA — WSJ: billions in guarantees to AI startups.** A Wall Street Journal investigation published Nov 13 (relayed Sunday by VideoCardz/aiagentupdate) reports Nvidia offered billions of dollars in financial guarantees to AI startups and small cloud providers that could not otherwise afford its chips, and that Huang privately described the incoming prepaid wafers and memory as an "avalanche." It is the circular-financing question in print — worth watching for on Tuesday's call, alongside Blackwell shipments and Q4 guidance.
- **NVDA / AAPL — analyst moves.** Bernstein cut NVDA to Market-Perform from Outperform and halved its target to $200; Oppenheimer downgraded to Perform and removed its target; Stifel kept Buy but cut its target to $205 from $315 (finwire.io weekly roundup). On the other side, Bernstein reiterated Outperform on AAPL with a $380 target, arguing Apple has become the safe haven for investors fleeing riskier AI names, spending just 2% of revenue on capex and running AI models on-device via its Google partnership.
- **Congress — chip export crackdown accelerating.** Lawmakers are pushing four AI chip-control bills toward the annual defense spending package after the Nvidia scandal, per Sunday reporting; the Senate track (Huang Dec 3, Lutnick Dec 10) runs in parallel.
- **Anthropic IPO — demand reportedly ~6x oversubscribed** (Sunday newsletter roundup). Pricing Wed Nov 18 ($70–78 range), trading from Thu Nov 19 under ANT — the week's other AI-sentiment test.
- **Macro/geopolitics — unchanged since mid-session.** Iran's claimed drone strike on a Bahrain base (Bloomberg/CBS) remains the oil-risk item for Monday's open; Bitcoin touched a three-month low Saturday before recovering to ~$65,700; the Fed's Nov 13 survey (87% of market contacts naming AI a major financial-system risk, up from 50% in spring) still frames the week.
