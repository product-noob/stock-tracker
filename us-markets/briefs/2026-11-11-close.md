## Why it moved

### The macro: hot CPI puts 50bp on the table
The BLS reported October CPI at 8:30 AM ET: headline +0.4% m/m (vs +0.3% consensus, down from +0.6% in September) and core +0.3% (vs +0.2% expected) — the third straight month at 0.3%. No year-on-year figures were published: the 2025 federal shutdown prevented October 2025 data collection, so there is no comparison base (Cleveland Fed nowcast put y/y at ~3.8%, up from 3.7% in September). Hours before the print, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in London that a core reading of 0.3% or more meant "a stronger policy move may be needed" and a 50bp hike "should be considered" at the Dec 8–9 meeting — adding that "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk." This is the last CPI print the Fed gets before December (November's lands the day after the meeting). After the data, Polymarket put 50bp-or-more at ~11% (from <1% a day earlier); a 25bp hike remains the clear favourite at ~85%. A December hike of some size is now near-certain. Rate-sensitive growth and chips bore the brunt; the move is macro, not a re-think of AI demand.

### NVDA — $168.95, -5.44% (holding)
Second day of the probe-driven slide: -9.5% in two sessions, ~30% below the Oct 6 record ($243.37), lowest close since late March, and now sitting just above the 52-week low of $164.27 (Mar 30). The headlines kept getting worse: The Information reported that Alice Huang — the Megaspeed executive at the centre of the affair — helped facilitate $7.5B+ of Nvidia GPU sales into China over several years (Reuters could not verify it); Bloomberg reported the FBI is investigating; the White House is weighing new export controls on Nvidia's advanced chips; and the Rosen Law Firm filed a shareholder class action in San Jose. Analysts moved the same day: Wells Fargo cut its target $315 → $300 (Overweight kept), Citi cut $315 → $285 (Buy kept), BofA removed NVDA from its US 1 highest-conviction list (Buy, $350 kept), and Aletheia cut to Hold and withdrew its $340 target. Dan Ives puts the chance of Huang being removed at ~15% and expects the board review to take 4–6 weeks. Volume ~2.2x average confirms real institutional selling, not drift. Nothing in any of this touches Blackwell demand or the Nov 17 earnings print.

**Koda's read:** Still a governance/leadership discount, not a business event — every target cut kept a Buy-equivalent rating, and even the reduced targets ($285–300) sit ~70% above the current price. But the overhang is now compounding (FBI, export controls, class action) and the stock is in a technical freefall toward its March low. **Lean: HOLD — do not add into the headline storm, and do not sell into panic either.** Watch $164.27 (52-week low) below and $170.24 (Monday's low) as the near-term test; $178.67 (Monday's close) is now resistance. Earnings Nov 17 is the next real catalyst — and the last one that can change the subject.
