## Tape: relief bounce faded into the afternoon

The morning was a clean relief bounce after Friday's rout (S&P 500 −1.82% to 7,580.07; Nasdaq −2.86% to 25,437; Dow −1.09% to 51,770.31 — Friday's worst day since June). Drivers: oil plunging on hopes for more US–Iran diplomacy time (Brent −~6% toward ~$107–108, WTI −~6% toward ~$97–98 intraday; Reuters via Kitco/Morningstar) and a better-than-expected ISM manufacturing print (see below). By midday the Motley Fool had the Dow +1.05% to 52,314, the S&P 500 +0.64% to 7,629 and the Nasdaq +0.76% to 25,631 (11:31 AM ET). Into the afternoon, the broad indices held gains (intraday prints: S&P 500 +~0.5–0.6%, Nasdaq +~0.7%, Dow +~0.8–1.0%), but the universe split: Amazon surged while Apple, Microsoft and the chip names faded — a stock-specific session, not a uniform bounce.

Macro: ISM manufacturing PMI rose to 55.5 in October (Reuters via Kitco), beating the 54.5 consensus and September's 54.5 — a three-month high, with new orders 55.5 and production up to 59, its highest since Nov 2021. Prices paid eased to 75.2 from 77.9, still high enough that Reuters notes inflation should stay above the Fed's 2% target into 2027. The 10-year yield slipped to ~4.93–4.94%.

## Koda's read

A two-speed day: the broad market bounced on oil and a strong ISM print, but the AI complex did not follow through — chips and Apple faded into the close, and only an analyst upgrade (AMZN) and a deeply oversold GOOGL held green. That says dip-buying is selective, not broad, and tonight's AAPL print is now the tape's next trigger: a soft December guide would likely drag the whole group again, while a firm guide plus resilient Services could finally give the bounce a second leg. Into Tuesday: US midterms + AMD after the close (chip read-through after Intel's rout).
