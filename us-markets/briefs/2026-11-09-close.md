## Weekend read — three new threads, none of them priced yet

**NVDA — Jensen Huang and a US intelligence memo (Financial Times, Nov 7).** The FT reported that US officials drafted a memo earlier this year, based on intelligence, stating that CEO Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, a former senior executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer whom US intelligence believes was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to gather information on Nvidia's AI chips and influence export decisions. The memo was reportedly circulated to a small group of senior officials before President Trump's May summit trip to China. The New York Times had earlier reported the Megaspeed angle (Oct 29) and on Nov 7 reported that New Matrix, a Chinese company the US Commerce Department authorised to handle Nvidia's advanced H200 chips, shows little sign of being a real business. What is established and what is not: no charges have been brought against anyone, no public finding of wrongdoing has been made, and the memo itself has not been published. Nvidia says Huang's meetings with Alice Huang were routine customer business, that only general business information is shared with cloud customers, and that its internal review found no wrongdoing and no evidence Megaspeed smuggled chips. Republican lawmakers have asked for a closed-door briefing with Huang on Nov 18, with a deadline for Nvidia to answer by Wednesday Nov 11 (CNBC). Nvidia shares closed Friday at $186.64 (+2.78%) hours before the FT report — Monday's open is the first market test, nine sessions before Q3 results on Nov 17. This is headline/governance risk, not an earnings or export-rule change — but it lands on a stock already down ~22% from its highs and under political scrutiny over China chip sales.

**TSM — the 3D DRAM story is now denied, not just unconfirmed.** A TSMC spokesperson told Tom's Hardware and Bloomberg on Nov 7 that "TSMC always focuses on its core business, semiconductor foundry" — a firmer line than the non-denial noted in yesterday's recap. The Information's Nov 6 report (TSMC drafting 3D DRAM plans at Nvidia's request, new fab or conversion of an existing plant) still stands as a report, but the company's public position is now a denial. Industry scepticism was already high: Winbond's chair said in-house DRAM development by TSMC is unlikely, and analysts note commercial 3D DRAM is years away even for memory incumbents. Friday's +6.33% TSM move was partly built on this story, so the denial is a small headwind into Monday — though the memory-shortage premise behind it (SK Hynix sold out through 2029, Samsung through 2027) is unchanged.

**GOOGL — $17bn Wyoming data centre (Google blog, Nov 5; Reuters).** Google confirmed its first Wyoming data centre, a long-term $17bn investment in Laramie County with Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, AEP and Related Digital, powered in part by a first-of-its-kind commercial-scale 702 MW fuel-cell development with carbon capture and storage (Bloom Energy cells, made in the US; AEP investing ~$4bn under a 20-year power purchase agreement, full operation targeted end-2028). Google says it will pay for 100% of the power it uses. The site (Project Tembo, 716 acres) was acquired from Crusoe, confirmed Nov 6. Context: the announcement came two days after voters backed data-centre limits in 40 of 41 ballot measures — Google is pushing ahead with the buildout while political resistance to it grows, the same tension behind the week's AI-trade repricing.

**Politics — Trump blames the midterms for the selloff.** In a Nov 8 Truth Social post, President Trump called the midterm results a "giant step back" and blamed them for a $3 trillion one-day stock loss (ABC News/AFP). Stephen Miller starts as White House chief of staff on Monday, replacing Susie Wiles. Weekend commentary (Barron's, Nov 8) expects volatility when futures reopen tonight after a week in which the AI trade, not just AI stocks, was repriced.

**Nothing new on the rest of the universe:** no fresh earnings, guidance, analyst actions or company news for AAPL, MSFT, META, ASML or AMZN surfaced on Sunday.
