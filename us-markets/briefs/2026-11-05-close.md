## Holdings

### AMZN — Amazon: $268.20, +6.98% (prev. close $250.69) — day's biggest gainer, watchlist name
- Range: $263.85–$271.49. Up ~7% and at a 3-month high (mid-session check).
- Why: the same thesis as the mid-session alert — Amazon's ~$220B 2026 capex plan was the biggest worry hanging over the stock after its Oct 29 earnings; if data-centre expansion slows (Texas governor-elect pledged a moratorium; voters in Ohio, Illinois, Maine and Florida passed local bans/restrictions, per Dow Jones via Morningstar), the cash-burn fear eases and free cash flow recovers. Reuters had it +7.4% intraday at one point. No fresh Amazon-specific news beyond the thesis.

### MSFT — Microsoft: $557.18, +6.95% (prev. close $520.99)
- Range: $542.30–$558.20. Above its $551.15 52-week high — a new high (mid-session check).
- Why: same gridlock/data-centre thesis, and Microsoft was already the exception in the group — $18.6B quarterly free cash flow in its Oct 28 print while peers went cash-negative. A slower buildout eases its capex burden without touching the Azure growth story. No fresh Microsoft-specific news.

### AAPL — Apple: $352.40, +6.18% (prev. close $331.90)
- Range: $340.99–$354.50. A record high (Reuters, via the mid-session check), reversing Tuesday's −1.9% post-earnings slide and then some.
- Why: two tailwinds in the same thesis — Apple has no hyperscaler capex trap, and if data-centre demand cools, the memory-price surge that pressured its December-quarter gross-margin guide (45–46% vs 47.9% in September) eases too. Evercore also flagged Apple's defensive characteristics in this tape.
- Analyst actions today (MarketBeat table): Goldman Sachs set a $380 price target; DZ Bank downgraded Hold → Sell with a $300 target — a sharp split that mirrors the margin debate. The margin guide itself is unchanged; today repriced the memory-cost risk, not the quarter.
- After the close: Qualcomm and Arm report — the smartphone-demand read for Apple's chain. Results were not in these prices.

### GOOGL — Alphabet: $291.43, +4.07% (prev. close $280.03)
- Range: $283.40–$296.49. Faded from a $296.49 intraday high and from its +5.4% mid-morning print, but still the fourth-biggest gainer in the universe.
- Why: same thesis, with arguably the most leverage to it — Q3 capex hit $51.4B with free cash flow at −$9.07B, the sharpest cash swing in the group. A slower buildout directly repairs that picture. Still far below its ~$336 50-day average, so this reads as a bounce from an oversold $280, not a trend flip. No fresh Alphabet-specific news.

## Watchlist

### META — Meta: $628.12, +1.14% (prev. close $621.03)
- Range: $624.50–$643.09. Faded from its $643.09 high and from +2.2% at mid-session — the clear laggard among the five gainers.
- Why: rides the same gridlock/data-centre thesis, but the market still doubts Meta's spend more than its peers' — Q3 showed the first negative free cash flow since 2012, plus the $9.9B settlement charge and the $25B New Mexico privacy-penalty appeal. The AMD divergence says the market is discriminating between AI winners and AI spenders, not buying the whole chain — and Nvidia, reporting November 17, is now the name carrying the most event risk into that tape. No Nvidia-specific news today.

## Koda's read

A 7% day on a pledge is not a re-rating — Texas's moratorium is promised, not enacted, and the December-quarter margin guide, the capex plans and the November 17 Nvidia print are all still ahead. The split itself is the useful signal: the market paid up for cash flow and sold the buildout. Until a moratorium becomes policy or Nvidia reports, treat today as a positioning day inside a correction, not the end of it.
