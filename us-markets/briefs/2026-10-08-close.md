## Benchmarks & macro

- **S&P 500 −0.2%** and **Nasdaq Composite −0.8%** (Investor's Business
  Daily, "The Big Picture"); both halted six-day winning streaks. Volume
  fell. **Dow ended modestly lower** (WSJ late-session; Reuters reported
  ~−0.6%/−325 pts at the open). Tale-of-two-markets: gold miners, airlines,
  oil stocks rose and blunted the S&P's fall, while tech led down.
- **FOMC minutes (Sept 15–16 meeting, released 2 PM ET): hawkish but
  priced in.** Unanimous 12-0 vote for the 25 bp hike to 3.75–4.00% (first
  hike in 3 years); "most participants" assessed another increase likely
  appropriate by year-end (WSJ, MarketWatch). Market reaction muted: CME
  FedWatch October-hike odds *fell* to 17.2% from 19.9% — the tape is
  pricing any next move in December (MarketWatch).
- **The 10Y auction at 1 PM ET was the session's stabilizer.** $39B sold
  at 5.30%; bid-to-cover 2.77 (strongest since 2014); primary dealers took
  only 2.5% — least since the financial crisis (Barron's citing BMO's
  Vail Hartman). This capped a morning spike: 10Y hit **5.368%** (highest
  since 2002), 30Y **5.724%** (24-year high) before the auction eased the
  10Y back toward ~5.27% (WSJ, TradingNews).
- **Oil above $100:** Brent ~$100–102 on Iran attacks on Saudi airports
  and Hormuz shipping (Reuters); WTI ~$90 (Investopedia). Energy was the
  second-biggest headwind after rates. September PPI came in hot today
  (per US-market chatter); CPI lands Thursday morning.
- Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell ~2.3% in the morning trade
  (Coinpaper citing intraday tape); ~73% of S&P 500 stocks were lower
  intraday — narrow records, broad weakness.

## Koda's read

A healthy-looking pause, not a reversal: the tape sold off on rates (10Y
at a 24-year high) and $100+ oil — the two things that punished
multiples all month — yet S&P 500 gave back only 0.2% and volume fell,
meaning this was de-risking, not distribution. The strong 10Y auction
was the real tell: real demand exists at 5.30%, and the market exhaled
after it. Two things stand out for our book: (1) the AI-capex flywheel
kept spinning even on a red day — SpaceX raising $40B *to buy Nvidia
chips* and Nvidia opening the PC socket with RTX Spark are demand-side
confirmations, not supply warnings; (2) META's −2.4% is the one move that
lacked a name-specific reason — if there's no news by tomorrow's
pre-market, treat it as rate-beta rather than a thesis crack, but don't
ignore a repeat. Nothing today changes the setup: the next genuine
information arrives at ASML (Oct 14) and TSM (Oct 15). No buys or sells
triggered; no sudden-move alerts fired today (no name moved ≥2% on a
fresh single catalyst — META's −2.4% had no catalyst, so per the rule it
