## Benchmarks & macro

- **Benchmarks:** S&P 500 **+0.6%**, Nasdaq Composite **+0.6%**,
  Dow Jones Industrial Average **+0.8% (+423 points)** (Barron's
  close wrap, "S&P 500 Closes Just Off Record High";
  Investopedia close wrap agrees: Dow +0.8%, Nasdaq +0.6%,
  S&P 500 +0.6%). Proxies at the bell: **QQQ $751.27, +0.49%**
  vs Thu $747.58 (Finnhub; FinanceCharts $751.25); **SPY ~$778.54**
  (FinanceCharts, 10/09/2026) vs Thu $773.43; SPX500 CFD 7,820.2,
  +0.53% vs 7,778.8 (Finnhub/OANDA). For the week: S&P 500 +1.1%,
  Dow +0.9%, Nasdaq +0.6% — the Nasdaq's fourth straight weekly
  gain (Investopedia). Read: a broad relief rebound, not a tech-only
  day — the Dow led, and the S&P closed just off its record.
- **The Thursday scare faded.** Friday's bid came as investors moved
  past the FT report that OpenAI's annualised revenue is running
  ~$50B, ~$20B below the ~$70B previously signalled. OpenAI's
  overnight pushback (still expects to reach/exceed $70B annualised
  by year-end; gap read as accounting vs demand) let hyperscalers
  and part of the chip complex repair (Barron's: "many technology
  stocks rebounded as investors moved past concerns over OpenAI's
  sluggish revenue"). The repair was selective, not blanket — see
  NVDA/TSM below.
- **Oil eased, yields held.** Brent ~$102.91–103.53 (−0.7% to −1.3%)
  and WTI ~$90.40–90.97 after President Trump said the US will not
  attack Iran before the Nov 3 midterm elections amid "productive
  discussions" (Reuters). Brent still held above $100 and finished
  the week higher after Thursday's +4% settle. The 10Y Treasury
  was ~5.24–5.26% — steady, just below Wednesday's 24-year high
  of 5.364% (Investopedia; Reuters midday). Gold ~$4,210 (+1%),
  bitcoin ~$83,000.
- **UMich sentiment missed — and the market shrugged.** Preliminary
  October sentiment 46.3 vs 47.8 forecast and 48.1 September final;
  current conditions fell 12.2% to 44.7, a record low, while
  expectations ticked up to 47.3. One-year inflation expectations
  rose to 4.7% (from 4.6%) and five-year to 3.5% (from 3.4%) —
  highest since May (Reuters; University of Michigan). Equities
  looked through it and traded the earnings calendar instead.
- **The day's violent tape was telecom, not tech.** SpaceX (+1.8%)
  agreed to acquire a nationwide low-band spectrum portfolio from
  Grain Management for Starlink Mobile. AT&T and Verizon each fell
  ~10% and T-Mobile ~12% — the S&P 500's three worst performers —
  while tower names ripped: Crown Castle +14%, American Tower
  +7.9%, SBA +6.2% (Barron's). Humana +11–13% on improved Medicare
  Advantage star ratings; Delta ended roughly flat after an early
  −1.5% on a fiscal Q3 miss and a cut full-year outlook (Barron's;
  Investopedia). Fastly +15% on an Oppenheimer upgrade; Lumentum
  +6.2% after management said optical components are effectively
  sold out through early 2029 (Barron's).

## Why per notable mover

**AMZN +3.29%, $262.43 — best in the universe, on positioning more
than print.** No fresh company-specific catalyst in US press on
Friday. The move extends Thursday's mid-session pattern: Dow
leadership on a broad large-cap rebound, with Tigress Financial's
Oct 6 target raise to $385 (from $315) on AWS/AI/advertising
momentum still the freshest bullish marker into Oct 29 earnings
(Q2 AWS revenue +37% YoY in this cycle's reporting). Friday's
close at the high ($262.80 high vs $262.43 close) after a $254.06
Thursday close says pre-earnings loading, not a reaction to news.

**MSFT +2.38%, $535.07 — analyst defence, cleared Thursday's high.**
Bernstein reiterated Outperform ($641 target), arguing the gap
between Microsoft's AI capex and revenue is timing, not
fundamentals, and framing the pullback as a buying opportunity;
a Goldman Sachs call characterising the software selloff as a rare
"value opportunity" added sector momentum (mid-session FinancialContent/
StockStory via Thursday's close brief; MEXC/Bernstein note summaries).
The stock opened $528.24, printed $537.22, and closed above
Thursday's $533.51 high — a clean reclaim. Earnings Oct 28 (estimate).

**AAPL −1.10%, $336.68 — the only holding with real bad news, and it
recovered off the low.** Nikkei Asia reported Apple told suppliers
to cut October component orders for the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max
by 15–20% from initial plans on softer-than-expected demand at
higher price points (Barron's: "Apple dropped 1.7%" intraday on
the report; shares fell ~2% premarket to $333.30 per the pre-market
brief). Morgan Stanley was reported calling the cuts "concerning"
in US midday roundups. The session low $330.70 (−2.9% from Thu
$340.42) held, and the close at $336.68 recovered roughly half the
intraday loss — with the Oct 13 smart-home ("Welcome Home") event
the next scheduled catalyst. Earnings Oct 29 (estimate). Hold-type
read, not an add-the-headline read (see Koda's read).

**NVDA −0.52%, $229.28 — the rebound that didn't hold.** NVDA opened
$233.84 (+1.5% vs Thu) with semis bid premarket (SOXX +~1.9% in the
pre-market brief), then faded all afternoon to close at $229.28,
near the $229.11 low. Barron's split the AI tape exactly this way:
"Some AI stocks rebounded… Others traded in the red: Advanced Micro
Devices, Intel, Marvell, and Sandisk all fell." NVDA landed in the
second group — still digesting the OpenAI revenue debate and
Thursday's −2.94% rather than leading the repair. GTC Berlin
Oct 20–22 and the Oct-23 $240 call interest flagged Thursday
remain the near-term positioning tells. Earnings ~Nov 19–26 (estimate).

**TSM −1.02%, $453.31 — a $15 fade from the open into earnings.**
TSM opened $464.21, tagged $464.83 in the first minutes, then sold
steadily to a $449.75 low and a $453.31 close. September sales
(+54.6% YoY, reported Oct 8) put Q3 revenue on track vs the
$44.6–45.8B guide, so Friday reads as profit-taking / de-risking
four sessions before the Oct 15 pre-market print (consensus EPS
~$4.42–4.45, revenue ~$45.4–45.5B) — margin and capex, not revenue,
are the questions, per the Oct 8 recap. No new TSM-specific
negative in US press on Friday.

## Koda's read

Friday undid the shape of Thursday without undoing the level:
Thursday was a chip-led, oil-and-yields selloff on one FT headline;
Friday was a broad, Dow-led relief (+0.8% Dow, +0.6% S&P/Nasdaq)
on light volume that reclaimed the indexes but not the chip
leadership — NVDA and TSM both opened up and closed red, which is
the tell that the AI complex is still being marked name-by-name
rather than bought as a basket. Two of our eight carried real,
name-specific information today and both were handled correctly
by the tape: AAPL's order-cut headline was sold to $330.70 and
then bought back half-way (bad news, orderly book, event support
on Oct 13 — no chase, no panic), while AMZN +3.3% and MSFT +2.4%
had no commensurate new facts — that's pre-earnings positioning
into Oct 14/15 (ASML/TSM), Oct 28/29 (META/MSFT/AAPL/AMZN), and
it should be respected as positioning, not chased as signal.
The macro that matters did not improve: UMich current conditions
at a record low with 1-year inflation expectations at 4.7% is a
stagflationary mix, Brent is still >$100, and the 10Y is still
~5.25% — the market simply chose to price earnings next week
instead. Nothing Friday changes the calendar: **CPI + ASML on
Wed Oct 14, TSM Thu Oct 15, FOMC Oct 28–29.** No buys or sells
triggered by today's tape; the NVDA/TSM opens-to-red fades are
logged here as positioning colour, not sudden-move alerts.
