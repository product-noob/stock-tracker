## Weekend read — the AI trade is being repriced, not just sold off

Bloomberg's weekend analysis (via Moneyweb, Nov 7) frames the week as a break in the AI-spending consensus rather than a one-day flush. The trigger was Tuesday's US midterms, in which Democrats took majorities in the Senate and House plus state houses and governorships — investors read that as new political risk to the data-centre buildout (permits, power, antitrust scrutiny all back on the table). Thursday's rout followed: SOX −12.25% and Nasdaq-100 −6.4%, the worst one-day drops for both since March 2020; NVDA −9.26% and more than $440B of market value in a day. UBS strategists estimate nearly one-third of the computing power projected to come online in the US in 2028 is now at risk. Valuations have compressed hard: NVDA now trades at ~13x estimated profits over the next 12 months, down from more than 32x a year ago; Broadcom ~15x vs a 42x peak in December. CoreWeave fell 20% this week and is down more than 75% from its June 2025 peak. The piece notes one upside to a slower buildout: hyperscalers (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) would be forced to curb spending — the same spending investors punished after the recent earnings prints.

**Microsoft–Oracle:** Friday's wrap (Binance/Sina) confirmed Microsoft is gradually winding down its arrangement to rent AI computing power from Oracle — the neocloud-oversight thread from Thursday is still live into Monday.
