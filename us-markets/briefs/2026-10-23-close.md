# Market-close recap — Thursday Oct 22, 2026 (4:00 PM ET)

**The AI-spend reckoning.** One day after SpaceX and Super Micro sparked an AI-fueled rally, the tape reversed hard: the **Nasdaq Composite fell ~2.1%**, the **Nasdaq-100 ~−2.9%** (QQQ proxy), and the **S&P 500 ~−1.4%** (SPY proxy). Reuters' framing: earnings from Tesla and Meta "did little to address AI-related concerns" — the market's focus shifted from earnings strength to the enormous capital required to build AI infrastructure and its uncertain return timeline. The selloff put the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) on track to shed **~$616B in market value** (−5.6%, lowest since July 29), with the Nasdaq-100 set to erase nearly $1T. Steve Sosnick (Interactive Brokers): investors are "reassessing the pace of AI spending and the returns that spending can generate."

Two tripwires fired inside the chip complex: **Lam Research reported a record quarter (Q3 revenue $8.22B, adjusted EPS $2.20, both beats) but signaled near-term supply constraints that capped its December-quarter outlook** — LRCX fell ~9–10% intraday, dragging Applied Materials ~−6% and the whole equipment group with it. The second: **META's −7% post-earnings sell-the-news** on the raised capex low end + new non-voting Class C shares, which turned Wednesday's +3% after-hours pop into a credibility question about the AI build-out's bill.

Oil compounded the risk-off: **Brent ~$112 (+2.5–3%, five-month high)** on more Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia, with US/Iran escalation fears (US personnel evacuations from the region; reporting that Israel and the US are preparing to resume strikes on Iran after the Nov 3 midterms). Flight-to-safety: **10Y slipped to ~5.03%**, below 5% intraday for the first time since late September. VIX ~18.4 (+~10%).

## Why — per name

**META −7.04% ($648.43)** — the post-earnings verdict came down hard. The quarter itself was the double beat (rev $64.6B, +26% YoY; GAAP EPS $3.83 after the $9.89B state-AG settlement accrual; Q4 guide $73–76B), but three things flipped the tape: (1) **2026 capex low end raised to $135B** — modest, but it confirmed the spend ratchet; (2) the **new non-voting Class C shares**, distributed one-for-one to Class A/B holders effective Jan 2027 — the market read it as dilution risk and a signal Meta will fund 2027's build with fresh paper, even with a 2-for-1 split effect attached; (3) the **$25B New Mexico privacy judgment** (Meta will appeal). Zuckerberg's side deal (sell up to half his stake while committing to stay in California 8 years) didn't calm it either. Silver lining per CNBC: Reality Labs 2027 losses to narrow to ~$15B vs the $18–19B 2026 range.
