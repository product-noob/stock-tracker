## Weekend news — what changed since Saturday's close brief

- **NVDA / Huang — deadline passed, no acceptance, no subpoena (yet).** The House's 8 PM ET Friday deadline for Jensen Huang to confirm in writing that he will sit for a Dec 1 closed-door transcribed interview passed with no confirmed acceptance and no subpoena issued, per ABC/CBS reporting summarised Sunday (nacionale.com). ABC said Friday afternoon the two sides were in "active negotiations." Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott still wants Huang in public on Dec 3 and Commerce Secretary Lutnick on Dec 10 — Lutnick has agreed to appear voluntarily. The board's independent probe (Wachtell Lipton's Jun Kim) and the FBI track continue; the board says it has not formally engaged any CEO candidate and is still selecting a search firm. Next fixed date: Tuesday's earnings, where investors will press interim CEO Colette Kress on the search.
- **TSM — 2nm pricing power, on the record.** TSMC is pricing 2-nanometer production at $30,000 per 300mm wafer — ~50% above 3nm — and held a record 72.5% of global foundry revenue in Q2 2026; Apple holds roughly half of initial 2nm capacity, with commercial deliveries starting H1 2027 (eastasiabrief.com, Nov 15). Separately, TSMC disclosed record overseas fab subsidies of NT$167.59B (~$5.1B) in the first nine months of 2026, +133% y/y, flowing to Arizona, Kumamoto, Dresden and Nanjing. Full-year results Jan 14, 2027. None of this changes Friday's $400 break — but it is a reminder the TSM sell-off is contagion, not fundamentals.
- **Ackman letter — the hyperscaler capex debate, in print.** Bill Ackman's Q3 Pershing Square letter (reported Nov 14, Yahoo Finance/Motley Fool) argues Wall Street misreads AMZN, MSFT, META and GOOGL: consensus treats the capex build-out as a permanent rise in maintenance intensity while assuming minimal revenue acceleration beyond this year and next. The numbers behind it: in Q3 2026 the five hyperscalers together generated roughly minus $0.6B of free cash flow (Apple +$26B, Microsoft +$19B, Alphabet -$9B, Meta -$1.2B — its first negative quarter in over a decade — Amazon -$35B), with combined capex estimated at ~$730B this year and ~$980B in 2027 (Fiscal.ai estimates via arvy). Worth reading before Tuesday, when NVDA's print is the next test of whether the spend is paying.
- **Mag 7 split.** NVDA and GOOGL are both ~29% below their 12-month highs and at or below their 200-day averages; META is ~18% below its high, sitting on the 200-day; AAPL closed at a record on Mon Nov 9, MSFT ~4% and AMZN ~5% below their highs, all three above the 200-day (arvy, Nov 15).
